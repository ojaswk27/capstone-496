# Rocket Recovery Systems
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Parachute Recovery

#### 1.1 Parachute Types

Round (hemispherical):
- C_D = 0.75-0.85
- Stable descent
- Simple construction
- Most common for model rockets

Cruciform:
- C_D = 0.6-0.7
- More stable than round
- Less packing volume
- Lower descent rate variation

X-form:
- C_D = 0.7-0.8
- Good stability
- Easy construction

Annular (toroidal):
- C_D = 0.85-0.95
- Higher drag
- More complex

Elliptical/Steerable:
- C_D = 0.7-0.8
- Can glide and steer
- For precision landing

#### 1.2 Descent Rate Calculation

Steady-state descent rate:
V_d = √(2 × W / (ρ × C_D × A))

Where:
- W = Weight (N)
- C_D = Drag coefficient
- A = Projected area (m²)
- ρ = Air density (kg/m³)

Target descent rates:
- Model rockets: 4-6 m/s
- High power: 6-10 m/s
- Fragile payloads: 3-5 m/s

#### 1.3 Parachute Sizing

Required diameter:
D = √(8 × W / (π × ρ × C_D × V_d²))

Example for 1 kg rocket, 5 m/s descent:
D = √(8 × 9.81 / (π × 1.225 × 0.8 × 25)) = 0.80 m

Safety factor: Size for 1.2-1.5× calculated weight

### 2. Ejection Systems

#### 2.1 Black Powder Ejection

Charge sizing:
m_BP = 0.5 to 2.5 g per cubic inch of volume

Formula (grams):
m_BP = k × D² × L

Where:
- k = 0.002-0.004 (empirical)
- D = Body tube diameter (mm)
- L = Section length (mm)

Typical charges:
- 18mm tube: 0.3-0.5 g
- 38mm tube: 0.8-1.2 g
- 54mm tube: 1.5-2.5 g
- 75mm tube: 2.5-4.0 g

#### 2.2 CO2 Ejection

Pressure required:
P = F_shear / A_bulkhead

Typical: 10-20 psi sufficient

CO2 systems:
- Punctured cartridge
- Reliable, reusable
- Temperature dependent

#### 2.3 Electronic Deployment

Altimeter-based:
- Deploy at apogee (main)
- Deploy at altitude (backup)
- Dual redundancy common

E-match ignition:
- Low current igniter
- Triggered by flight computer
- Typical: 1-2 A fire current

Typical dual-deploy sequence:
1. Motor burnout detection
2. Apogee detection (accelerometer/barometer)
3. Drogue deployment
4. Main deployment at 200-300 m AGL

### 3. Drogue and Main Deployment

#### 3.1 Dual Deployment Benefits

Reduces drift:
- High descent rate above main deploy
- Lower drift in high winds

Reduces opening shock:
- Drogue slows vehicle first
- Main opens at lower speed

Typical descent rates:
- Drogue: 15-25 m/s
- Main: 4-8 m/s

#### 3.2 Drogue Sizing

Size for target descent rate:
D_drogue = √(8 × W / (π × ρ × C_D × V_drogue²))

Typical drogue: 1/10 to 1/5 of main area

Opening shock consideration:
- Drogue must survive high-speed deployment
- Heavier construction than main

#### 3.3 Deployment Sequence

At apogee:
1. Detect apogee (zero vertical velocity)
2. Ejection charge fires
3. Drogue deploys
4. Vehicle descends at ~20 m/s

At main altitude:
1. Detect altitude (300-500 m typical)
2. Second ejection charge fires
3. Main parachute deploys
4. Vehicle descends at ~5 m/s

### 4. Streamer Recovery

#### 4.1 Streamer Design

Recommended dimensions:
- Length: 10-20× body diameter
- Width: 1-2× body diameter
- Material: Mylar, plastic, ripstop nylon

Drag coefficient:
C_D ≈ 0.1-0.2 (depending on flutter)

Descent rate:
Higher than parachute (10-20 m/s)

#### 4.2 Applications

Suitable for:
- Light rockets (<100 g)
- Small rockets where parachute impractical
- Windy conditions (less drift)
- Tumble recovery combination

Not suitable for:
- Heavy or fragile payloads
- Rough terrain landing sites

### 5. Alternative Recovery Methods

#### 5.1 Featherweight Recovery

Concept:
- Ultra-light construction
- Low ballistic coefficient
- Tumbles to slow descent

C_D of tumbling cylinder: 1.0-1.5

For light rockets (<50 g):
Can achieve safe descent rates without parachute

#### 5.2 Helicopter (Rotor) Recovery

Auto-rotating blades:
- Fold during ascent
- Deploy at apogee
- Spin to slow descent

Descent rate:
V_d ≈ √(W / (ρ × A_disk × C_T))

More complex but reusable

#### 5.3 Glide Recovery

Folding wings:
- Stored during boost
- Deploy after burnout
- Glide to landing

Requires:
- Stability system
- Deployment mechanism
- Possibly guidance

### 6. Shock Cord Design

#### 6.1 Material Selection

Rubber (surgical tubing):
- Elastic, absorbs shock
- Degrades with UV and heat
- Short life

Tubular nylon:
- Strong, heat resistant
- Less stretch than rubber
- Longer life

Kevlar:
- Very strong, heat resistant
- No stretch
- Needs shock absorber

#### 6.2 Length Sizing

Minimum length:
L_shock = 3-5× body tube length

Elastic shock cord:
L_min = 2× separation distance

Purpose:
- Absorb deployment shock
- Prevent parts collision
- Keep components together

#### 6.3 Strength Requirements

Breaking strength:
F_break > k × W × g × (V_deploy / V_d)²

Where k = 5-10 safety factor

Typical strengths:
- 3mm tubular nylon: 150 kg
- 6mm tubular nylon: 400 kg
- Kevlar 200: 90 kg

### 7. Landing Analysis

#### 7.1 Impact Energy

Kinetic energy at landing:
KE = 0.5 × m × V_d²

Safe landing:
KE/A < threshold (depends on structure)

Target: KE < 30-50 J for fragile components

#### 7.2 Drift Distance

Horizontal drift:
D = V_wind × t_descent = V_wind × h / V_d

Example: 10 m/s wind, 500 m altitude, 5 m/s descent
D = 10 × (500/5) = 1000 m

Reducing drift:
- Higher descent rate (drogue)
- Deploy main lower
- Choose calm conditions

#### 7.3 Ground Impact

Impact force (simplified):
F = m × V² / (2 × s)

Where s = stopping distance

Soft ground (s = 0.1 m):
F = 0.5 × 1 × 25 / 0.1 = 125 N

Hard ground (s = 0.01 m):
F = 1250 N
