Each letter doubles the maximum impulse.

#### 1.2 Motor Designation Code

Format: X##-T or X###-T

Example: C6-3
- C = Impulse class (5.01-10.0 N·s)
- 6 = Average thrust (6 Newtons)
- 3 = Delay time (3 seconds)

Example: F39-6
- F = Impulse class (40.01-80.0 N·s)
- 39 = Average thrust (39 Newtons)
- 6 = Delay time (6 seconds)

Special designations:
- -0: No delay (booster motor)
- -P: Plugged (no ejection charge)

#### 1.3 Propellant Types

Black powder:
- Classes: 1/4A through D
- Impulse range: Up to 20 N·s
- Simple, reliable
- Lower performance

Composite (APCP):
- Classes: D through O
- Higher performance
- More expensive
- Greater variety

Hybrid:
- Liquid oxidizer, solid fuel
- Throttleable
- Complex but safe
- Growing popularity

### 2. Thrust Profiles

#### 2.1 Thrust Curve Shapes

Average thrust burn:
- Constant thrust throughout
- Example: Most Estes motors
- Good for general use

Progressive burn:
- Thrust increases over time
- Good for heavy rockets
- Prevents overspeed at burnout

Regressive burn:
- Thrust decreases over time
- Common for long-burn motors
- Gentle flight profile

Boost-sustain:
- High initial thrust, lower sustain
- Fast off the pad
- Example: Aerotech "J" suffix

#### 2.2 Burn Time

Burn time calculation:
t_b = I_total / F_avg

Example: C6-3
- I_total = 10 N·s (max for C)
- F_avg = 6 N
- t_b = 10/6 = 1.67 seconds

Short burn (< 1 s):
- High acceleration
- Good for lightweight rockets
- Reaches speed quickly

Long burn (> 2 s):
- Lower acceleration
- Better for heavy rockets
- More gradual speed buildup

### 3. Motor Selection

#### 3.1 Thrust-to-Weight Ratio

Minimum TWR for stable flight:
TWR_min = 5:1 (recommended)
TWR_min = 4:1 (absolute minimum)

Calculation:
TWR = F_avg / (W_rocket × g)

Example:
Rocket weight: 100 g = 0.98 N
Required thrust: 5 × 0.98 = 4.9 N minimum
Select motor with F_avg ≥ 5 N

#### 3.2 Delay Time Selection

Optimal delay ≈ Time from burnout to apogee

Estimation formula:
t_delay = v_burnout / g (simplified)

More accurate:
t_delay = v_b/g - (D_coast/m)/v_b (with drag)

General guidelines:
| Weight | Drag | Delay |
|--------|------|-------|
| Light | Low | Long (5-7s) |
| Light | High | Medium (3-5s) |
| Heavy | Low | Short (3-4s) |
| Heavy | High | Very short (0-3s) |

#### 3.3 Altitude Optimization

For maximum altitude:
- Use longest burn time practical
- Match motor to rocket weight
- Consider drag

Impulse vs altitude (rough):
- A motor: 30-100 m
- B motor: 60-200 m
- C motor: 120-400 m
- D motor: 200-600 m
- E motor: 300-900 m
- F motor: 400-1200 m
- G motor: 600-2000 m

### 4. Multi-Stage Motors

#### 4.1 Staging Concepts

Booster motor:
- Ignites at launch
- No delay (0 delay)
- Direct ignition of upper stage

Upper stage motor:
- Ignited by booster exhaust
- Normal delay for coast

#### 4.2 Stage Separation

Direct ignition staging:
- Booster burns out
- Hot gases ignite upper motor
- Upper stage pulls away

Gap staging:
- Small gap between motors
- Prevents premature ignition
- More reliable separation

#### 4.3 Multi-Stage Calculations

Total impulse:
I_total = I_booster + I_upper

Effective average thrust:
Different for each stage

Altitude gain:
h_total = h_boost + h_coast1 + h_upper + h_coast2

### 6. Safety Considerations

#### 6.1 Motor Handling

Storage:
- Cool, dry location
- Away from heat sources
- Original packaging

Before flight:
- Inspect for damage
- Check expiration date
- Verify correct motor

#### 6.2 CATO Prevention

Common causes of motor failure:
- Moisture damage
- Physical damage
- Improper installation
- Wrong delay

Prevention:
- Fresh motors
- Proper storage
- Correct mounting
- Follow instructions

#### 6.3 Certification Requirements

Low power (A-D):
- No certification needed
- NAR/TRA membership recommended

Mid power (E-G):
- No FAA waiver usually
- NAR/TRA membership recommended

High power (H and above):
- Certification required
- Level 1: H-I motors
- Level 2: J-L motors
- Level 3: M and above
- FAA waiver may be required

#### 7.2 Motor Retention

Friction fit:
- Tape wrap for snug fit
- Simple but can fail
- OK for low power

Engine hook:
- Spring clip retention
- Standard for model rockets
- Reliable, reusable

Threaded closure:
- Screw-on retainer
- Standard for high power
- Most secure
