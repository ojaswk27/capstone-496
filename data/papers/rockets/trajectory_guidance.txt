# Rocket Trajectory Analysis and Guidance
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Vertical Flight Analysis

#### 1.1 Equations of Motion

Vertical ascent equation:
m × dv/dt = T - D - m × g

Where:
- T = Thrust
- D = Drag = 0.5 × ρ × v² × Cd × A
- m = Instantaneous mass = m_0 - ṁ × t

Mass flow rate:
ṁ = T / (g_0 × I_sp)

#### 1.2 Burnout Conditions

Burnout velocity (vacuum, no gravity):
v_b = I_sp × g_0 × ln(m_0/m_f)

Burnout velocity (with gravity loss):
v_b = I_sp × g_0 × ln(m_0/m_f) - g × t_b

Burnout altitude:
h_b = ∫[0 to t_b] v(t) dt

#### 1.3 Coast Phase

After burnout, ballistic trajectory:
v(t) = v_b - g × (t - t_b)

Altitude:
h(t) = h_b + v_b × (t - t_b) - 0.5 × g × (t - t_b)²

Apogee:
h_apogee = h_b + v_b² / (2 × g)

Time to apogee:
t_apogee = t_b + v_b / g

### 2. Gravity Turn Trajectory

#### 2.1 Gravity Turn Concept

Rocket gradually pitches over due to gravity.
No active steering required after initial pitch.
Efficient path to orbit.

Pitch angle evolution:
dγ/dt = -(g × cos(γ)) / v

Where γ is the flight path angle.

#### 2.2 Optimal Pitch Program

Initial vertical rise: 100-500 m
Pitch kick: 1-5° from vertical
Gravity turn: Passive trajectory

Final pitch at burnout depends on mission:
- Suborbital: γ = 45-60°
- LEO: γ ≈ 0° (horizontal)

#### 2.3 Atmospheric Considerations

Maximum dynamic pressure (max-Q):
q_max = 0.5 × ρ × v²

Typical values:
- Sounding rockets: 30-50 kPa
- Orbital rockets: 30-40 kPa

Throttle back through max-Q to limit loads.

### 3. Orbital Injection

#### 3.1 Required Velocity

Circular orbit velocity:
v_orbit = √(μ/r) = √(g × R_earth × R_earth/r)

At 200 km: v_orbit = 7,784 m/s
At 400 km: v_orbit = 7,669 m/s

#### 3.2 Velocity Losses

Gravity loss:
Δv_gravity = ∫ g × sin(γ) dt

Typical: 1,000-1,500 m/s

Drag loss:
Δv_drag = ∫ (D/m) dt

Typical: 50-200 m/s

Steering loss:
Δv_steer = 50-200 m/s

Total Δv to LEO:
Δv_total = v_orbit + Δv_gravity + Δv_drag + Δv_steer
Δv_total ≈ 9,200-9,500 m/s

#### 3.3 Injection Accuracy

Required accuracy for LEO:
- Velocity: ±10 m/s
- Flight path angle: ±0.1°
- Position: ±1 km

Guidance system must achieve these tolerances.

### 4. Guidance Algorithms

#### 4.1 Explicit Guidance

Pre-programmed pitch schedule:
γ(t) = f(t) [open-loop]

Simple but not optimal for varying conditions.
Used for early rockets and model rockets.

#### 4.2 Iterative Guidance

Computes required thrust direction in real-time.
Converges to optimal trajectory.

Basic algorithm:
1. Predict burnout conditions
2. Compare to target
3. Adjust steering commands
4. Repeat

#### 4.3 Powered Explicit Guidance (PEG)

Used by Space Shuttle, many modern rockets.

Steering law:
λ = λ_0 + λ̇ × t

Where λ is the thrust direction.

Computes λ_0 and λ̇ to reach target state.

Terminal guidance:
- Position: r_target
- Velocity: v_target
- Flight path angle: γ_target

### 5. Atmospheric Drag

#### 5.1 Drag Coefficient

Subsonic (M < 0.8):
Cd ≈ 0.2-0.4 (streamlined)

Transonic (0.8 < M < 1.2):
Cd increases rapidly

Supersonic (M > 1.2):
Cd ≈ 0.3-0.6

Hypersonic (M > 5):
Cd decreases slowly

#### 5.2 Drag Model

Drag force:
D = 0.5 × ρ × v² × Cd × A_ref

Altitude dependence through ρ:
ρ = ρ_0 × exp(-h/H)

Where H ≈ 8,500 m (scale height).

#### 5.3 Drag Loss Minimization

Strategies:
- High thrust-to-weight (shorter time in atmosphere)
- Optimal trajectory (balance gravity and drag)
- Streamlined shape

### 6. Model Rocket Trajectory

#### 6.1 Simplified Analysis

Assumptions:
- Constant thrust during burn
- Negligible drag (for quick estimate)
- Constant gravity

Burnout velocity:
v_b = (T/m_avg - g) × t_b

Where m_avg is average mass during burn.

Coast altitude:
h_coast = v_b² / (2g)

Total altitude:
h_total = h_burn + h_coast

#### 6.2 Including Drag

Drag significantly affects altitude.

With drag, use numerical integration:
dv/dt = (T - D)/m - g
dh/dt = v

Drag reduces altitude by 20-50% for typical designs.

#### 6.3 Stability During Flight

Weathercock stability:
CP aft of CG by 1-2 calibers

Overcorrection if too stable (weathercocking).

Optimal stability margin:
SM = 1.5-2.0 calibers

### 7. Recovery System

#### 7.1 Parachute Sizing

Descent rate equation:
v_d = √(2 × W / (ρ × Cd × A_chute))

Target descent rate: 4-7 m/s

Parachute drag coefficient:
- Flat circular: Cd = 0.75-0.8
- Hemispherical: Cd = 0.9-1.2
- Cross-form: Cd = 0.6-0.8

Required parachute area:
A = 2 × W / (ρ × Cd × v_d²)

#### 7.2 Deployment Altitude

High altitude deployment:
- Longer descent time
- More drift with wind
- Gentler deceleration

Low altitude deployment:
- Higher opening loads
- Less drift
- Faster recovery

Optimal: 50-200 m for model rockets

#### 7.3 Ejection Charge Sizing

Black powder charge:
m_bp = P × V / (C × T × R)

Where:
- P = Desired pressure (10-15 psi)
- V = Ejection volume
- C = Gas constant factor
- T = Temperature
- R = Gas constant

Typical: 0.5-2.0 grams for model rockets

### 8. Sounding Rocket Missions

#### 8.1 Altitude Requirements

Different experiments require different altitudes:
- 80-120 km: Mesosphere studies
- 120-300 km: Ionosphere research
- 300-1000 km: Radiation belt studies
- >1000 km: Astronomy

#### 8.2 Payload Time

Time above altitude h:
t_above = 2 × √(2 × (h_apogee - h) / g)

For 100 km apogee, time above 80 km ≈ 4 minutes

Microgravity time:
t_μg = 2 × h_apogee / v_max

| Rocket | Altitude (km) | Payload (kg) |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Black Brant V | 150 | 70 |
| Terrier-Orion | 200 | 40 |
| Nike-Orion | 150 | 50 |
