# Rocket Structures and Materials
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Structural Design Requirements

#### 1.1 Load Cases

Flight loads:
- Axial acceleration: 3-10g typical
- Lateral (bending): 1-3g from wind, steering
- Max-Q: Maximum dynamic pressure region

Ground handling:
- Transportation: 2-3g
- Lifting: Point loads at attachment
- Stacking: Assembly loads

Pressure loads:
- Tank pressure: 1.5-5 MPa typical
- External pressure at max-Q
- Thermal cycling

#### 1.2 Safety Factors

Yield factor of safety: 1.1-1.25
Ultimate factor of safety: 1.25-1.5

For pressure vessels:
- Proof pressure: 1.25 × MEOP
- Burst pressure: 1.5 × MEOP

Where MEOP = Maximum Expected Operating Pressure

### 2. Body Tube Design

#### 2.1 Thin-Wall Cylinder Analysis

Hoop stress (internal pressure):
σ_h = p × r / t

Axial stress (pressure + thrust):
σ_a = p × r / (2t) + F / (2πrt)

Combined stress (von Mises):
σ_vm = √(σ_h² + σ_a² - σ_h × σ_a)

#### 2.2 Buckling

Axial compression buckling:
σ_cr = 0.6 × E × t/r (theoretical)
σ_cr = γ × 0.6 × E × t/r (with knockdown γ = 0.3-0.7)

External pressure buckling:
p_cr = 0.92 × E × (t/r)^2.5 / ((L/r) × (1-ν²)^0.75)

Combined loading interaction:
(σ/σ_cr) + (p/p_cr)² ≤ 1

#### 2.3 Ring and Stringer Stiffening

Ring spacing for shell stability:
L/r < 1.7 × (r/t)^0.5 (approximate)

Stiffener requirements:
- Prevent general instability
- Local buckling of panels
- Attachment strength

### 3. Propellant Tanks

#### 3.1 Tank Configurations

Cylindrical with domes:
- Most common
- Ellipsoidal domes (2:1 typical)
- Hemispherical domes (heavier but stronger)

Common bulkhead:
- Separates oxidizer and fuel
- Reduces length and weight
- Complex insulation requirements

Spherical:
- Minimum weight for given volume
- Maximum structural efficiency
- Packaging challenges

#### 3.2 Tank Materials

Aluminum alloys:
- 2219-T87: High strength, weldable
- 2014-T6: Good cryogenic properties
- 7075-T73: High strength (not weldable)

Aluminum-lithium:
- 2195: Lighter than standard Al
- 8% density reduction
- Space Shuttle External Tank

Composite overwrapped:
- Carbon/epoxy over metal liner
- 30-50% weight savings
- COPVs for high-pressure gas

Steel:
- 304L stainless: Cryogenic compatible
- Maraging steel: Very high strength
- Heavy but reliable

#### 3.3 Tank Sizing

Required volume:
V = m_prop / ρ_prop

Cylindrical section length:
L_cyl = (V - V_domes) / (π × r²)

Mass (cylindrical tank with domes):
m_tank = 2πr × t × (L_cyl + 2k_dome × r) × ρ_material

Where k_dome accounts for dome geometry.

Tank mass fraction:
m_tank / m_prop = (2t/r) × (ρ_material/ρ_prop) × geometry_factor

### 4. Interstage Structures

#### 4.1 Loads

Axial: Upper stage thrust + weight
Bending: Steering loads, wind loads
Torsion: Roll control, asymmetric thrust

#### 4.2 Configurations

Skin-stringer:
- Aluminum sheets with longerons
- Traditional construction
- Moderate weight

Monocoque:
- Pure shell structure
- Simplest to manufacture
- Higher weight

Isogrid:
- Machined integral stiffeners
- Triangular pattern
- High efficiency, high cost

Composite sandwich:
- CFRP skins with honeycomb core
- Excellent stiffness-to-weight
- Thermal considerations needed

### 5. Fins and Aerodynamic Surfaces

#### 5.1 Loading

Aerodynamic load:
F = C_N × q × A_ref

Where:
- C_N = Normal force coefficient
- q = Dynamic pressure = 0.5 × ρ × V²
- A_ref = Reference area

Fin flutter:
- Bending-torsion coupling
- Avoid flutter speed in flight envelope
- Stiffness and mass balance critical

#### 5.2 Materials

Model rockets:
- Balsa wood: Light, easy to shape
- Plywood: Stronger, heavier
- Plastic: Injection molded

High-power:
- Fiberglass: Good strength, moderate cost
- Carbon fiber: Best strength-to-weight
- Aluminum: Heat resistant

### 6. Nozzle Structures

#### 6.1 Thermal Environment

Combustion temperature: 2500-3500 K
Wall heat flux: 1-50 MW/m²
Nozzle expansion: Temperature drops

#### 6.2 Cooling Methods

Ablative:
- Material sacrificially removes heat
- Carbon-phenolic, silica-phenolic
- Simple, reliable, single-use

Regenerative:
- Fuel flows through cooling channels
- Efficient but complex
- Reusable engines

Film cooling:
- Propellant injected along walls
- Reduces heat transfer
- Some performance loss

Radiation cooling:
- High-temperature material radiates heat
- Refractory metals, ceramics
- Limited to low heat flux regions

#### 6.3 Nozzle Materials

Ablatives:
- Carbon-phenolic: Best performance
- Silica-phenolic: Lower cost
- Rubber-based: Easy processing

Metals:
- Copper alloys: High conductivity (regen)
- Inconel: High temperature strength
- Niobium/columbium: Radiation cooled

Ceramics:
- Carbon-carbon: Highest temperature
- SiC: Oxidation resistant
- CMCs: Emerging technology

### 7. Payload Fairings

#### 7.1 Requirements

Aerodynamic:
- Low drag coefficient
- Smooth contours
- Nose shape optimization

Acoustic:
- Payload protection
- Blankets and damping
- Vent design

Thermal:
- Aerodynamic heating protection
- Payload temperature limits
- Insulation design

#### 7.2 Separation

Pyrotechnic:
- Linear shaped charge
- Frangible joint
- Thrusters for kick

Clean separation:
- No debris
- Low shock
- Reliable deployment

### 8. Model Rocket Materials

#### 8.1 Body Tubes

Paper/cardboard:
- Spiral wound
- Light, cheap
- Low strength

Fiberglass:
- Filament wound or laid up
- Strong, heat resistant
- Standard for high-power

Carbon fiber:
- Highest strength-to-weight
- Premium cost
- May need RF transparent section

Phenolic:
- Paper-phenolic tubes
- Good strength
- Moderate cost

#### 8.2 Nose Cones

Plastic:
- Injection molded
- Low cost, consistent
- Model rockets

Fiberglass:
- Laid up or molded
- High power standard
- Various shapes available

Turned (machined):
- Wood, plastic, or metal
- Custom shapes
- Scale models

### 9. Weight Optimization

#### 9.1 Mass Ratios

Structural coefficient:
ε = m_structure / (m_structure + m_propellant)

Typical values:
- Solid rockets: 0.08-0.15
- Liquid rockets: 0.05-0.12
- Advanced stages: 0.03-0.08

#### 9.2 Mass Breakdown

Typical liquid stage:
| Component | % of dry mass |
|-----------|---------------|
| Tanks | 30-40% |
| Engines | 20-30% |
| Structure | 15-25% |
| Systems | 10-20% |
| Margin | 5-10% |
