# UAV Sensors and Navigation Systems
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Inertial Measurement Units (IMU)

#### 1.1 MEMS Sensors

Accelerometer specifications:
- Range: ±2g to ±16g
- Resolution: 0.1-1 mg
- Noise density: 50-200 μg/√Hz
- Bandwidth: 100-1000 Hz

Gyroscope specifications:
- Range: ±250 to ±2000 °/s
- Resolution: 0.01-0.1 °/s
- Noise density: 0.005-0.05 °/s/√Hz
- Bias stability: 1-10 °/hr

Common IMU chips:
- MPU-6050: Budget, 6-axis
- ICM-20689: Mid-range, low noise
- BMI088: High-performance

#### 1.2 Sensor Fusion

Complementary filter:
θ = α × (θ + ω×dt) + (1-α) × θ_accel

Where α = τ/(τ + dt), τ = 0.5-2.0 s typically

Madgwick filter:
- Gradient descent optimization
- Quaternion representation
- Computationally efficient

Extended Kalman filter:
- State: [q, bias_gyro, position, velocity]
- Fuses IMU + GPS + barometer
- Higher computational cost

### 2. GPS and GNSS

#### 2.1 GPS Receivers

Standard GPS:
- Horizontal accuracy: 2-5 m CEP
- Vertical accuracy: 5-10 m
- Update rate: 1-10 Hz

Dual-frequency (L1/L2):
- Horizontal accuracy: 1-2 m
- Better multipath rejection

RTK GPS:
- Horizontal accuracy: 1-2 cm
- Requires base station
- Update rate: up to 20 Hz

#### 2.2 Integration with IMU

Loosely coupled:
- GPS provides position/velocity
- INS provides attitude
- Separate filters

Tightly coupled:
- GPS pseudoranges used directly
- Single Kalman filter
- Better performance in degraded GPS

#### 2.3 GPS Specifications

Common modules:
- u-blox NEO-M8N: Standard, 10 Hz
- u-blox ZED-F9P: RTK capable
- Here+ RTK: Complete solution

Position update latency: 100-200 ms typical
Cold start time: 25-35 seconds
Hot start time: 1-2 seconds

### 3. Barometric Altitude

#### 3.1 Barometer Sensors

Pressure range: 300-1100 hPa (typical)
Resolution: 0.01-0.1 hPa
Noise: 0.1-1 Pa RMS
Temperature sensitivity: Compensated

Altitude from pressure:
h = 44330 × (1 - (p/p_0)^0.19)

Accuracy: ±0.5-2 m after calibration

Common sensors:
- BMP280: Budget, ±1 m
- MS5611: High resolution, ±10 cm
- BMP388: Low noise, ±8 cm

#### 3.2 Altitude Hold

Altitude Kalman filter:
- State: [altitude, vertical_velocity, bias]
- Measurements: Barometer, accelerometer
- Typical hold accuracy: ±0.5 m

### 4. Magnetometer (Compass)

#### 4.1 Specifications

Range: ±4 to ±16 Gauss
Resolution: 0.1-1 mGauss
Noise: 0.5-5 mGauss RMS

Earth's field: 25-65 μT (0.25-0.65 Gauss)

Common sensors:
- HMC5883L: Legacy, popular
- QMC5883L: Compatible replacement
- IST8310: Low noise

#### 4.2 Calibration

Hard iron correction:
B_corrected = B_measured - B_offset

Soft iron correction:
B_corrected = A × (B_measured - B_offset)

Where A is a 3×3 correction matrix

Calibration procedure:
1. Rotate drone in all orientations
2. Collect sphere of data points
3. Fit ellipsoid to data
4. Calculate correction parameters

### 5. Rangefinders

#### 5.1 Ultrasonic

Range: 0.2-5 m
Resolution: 1-5 cm
Beam width: 15-45°
Update rate: 10-50 Hz

Affected by temperature:
c_sound = 331 + 0.6 × T (m/s)

Common sensors:
- HC-SR04: Budget, 2-400 cm
- MaxBotix: Weatherproof, 5 m

#### 5.2 Infrared

Range: 0.1-1.5 m (analog)
Range: 0.1-8 m (digital lidar)
Resolution: 1-5 cm
Beam width: 1-5°

Common sensors:
- Sharp GP2Y0A21: 10-80 cm
- VL53L0X: Lidar, 0-2 m
- TFmini: Lidar, 0.3-12 m

#### 5.3 Lidar

Single point:
- Range: 0-40 m typical
- Resolution: 1 cm
- Update rate: 100-1000 Hz

Scanning (2D/3D):
- RPLidar A1: 360°, 12 m, 8000 pts/s
- Velodyne: 3D, 100 m, 300k pts/s

### 6. Optical Flow

#### 6.1 Operating Principle

Velocity estimation from visual motion:
v_x = (dx/dt) × h / f
v_y = (dy/dt) × h / f

Where:
- dx, dy = Pixel displacement
- h = Height above ground
- f = Focal length

#### 6.2 Specifications

Resolution: 30×30 to 256×256 pixels
Update rate: 50-200 Hz
Range: 0.1-10 m (with rangefinder)

Common sensors:
- PMW3901: 35×35 px, 240 Hz
- PX4Flow: 64×64 px, integrated sonar
- Mateksys 3901-L0X: Complete module

#### 6.3 Limitations

Requires:
- Texture on ground
- Altitude measurement
- Good lighting

Fails with:
- Uniform surfaces
- Very low altitude
- Excessive motion blur

### 7. Visual Navigation

#### 7.1 Camera Types

Global shutter:
- No rolling shutter distortion
- Higher cost
- Preferred for navigation

Rolling shutter:
- Less expensive
- Distortion during fast motion
- Requires compensation

#### 7.2 Visual Odometry

Monocular:
- Single camera
- Scale ambiguity
- Needs initialization

Stereo:
- Two cameras, known baseline
- Direct depth measurement
- Higher processing load

RGB-D:
- Color + depth (structured light or ToF)
- Indoor use primarily

#### 7.3 SLAM

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping:
- Build map while navigating
- EKF-SLAM, FastSLAM, ORB-SLAM
- Computationally intensive

### 8. Integration Architecture

#### 8.1 Sensor Redundancy

Triple redundancy:
- 3× IMU for voting logic
- 2× GPS for cross-checking
- Multiple altimeters

Failover logic:
- Primary → Secondary → Emergency

#### 8.2 Update Rates

| Sensor | Rate (Hz) | Latency (ms) |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| IMU | 250-1000 | 1-4 |
| Barometer | 50-100 | 10-20 |
| GPS | 5-20 | 100-200 |
| Compass | 50-100 | 5-10 |
| Optical flow | 50-200 | 5-20 |
| Lidar | 100-500 | 2-10 |

#### 8.3 Data Fusion Hierarchy

High rate (1000 Hz): IMU integration
Medium rate (100 Hz): Attitude filter
Low rate (10-50 Hz): Position filter
