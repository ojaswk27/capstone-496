# VTOL and Hybrid Aircraft Design
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. VTOL Configuration Types

#### 1.1 Tiltrotor
- Rotors tilt from vertical to horizontal
- Examples: V-22 Osprey, AW609
- High cruise speed capability
- Complex mechanism

Conversion corridor:
- Minimum speed for wing-borne flight
- Maximum speed for rotor-borne flight
- Overlap region for safe transition

#### 1.2 Tiltwing
- Entire wing tilts with rotors
- Better hover efficiency than tiltrotor
- Higher download in hover
- Examples: XC-142, LTV XC-142A

#### 1.3 Lift + Cruise (Compound)
- Separate lift and cruise propulsion
- Lift rotors for VTOL only
- Wings and pusher for cruise
- Examples: Many eVTOL designs

Lift rotor sizing:
P_hover = W^1.5 / (FM × √(2×ρ×A_lift))

#### 1.4 Vectored Thrust
- Jet thrust direction changes
- Complexity in nozzle design
- Examples: Harrier, F-35B

Hover thrust requirement:
T_hover > W × 1.05 (margin for control)

#### 1.5 Quadplane
- Fixed-wing with quad rotors
- Simple, popular for UAVs
- Rotors may fold or stop in cruise
- Examples: Many commercial UAVs

### 2. Hover Performance

#### 2.1 Disk Loading Comparison

| Configuration | Disk Loading (N/m²) |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Helicopter | 200-500 |
| Tiltrotor | 500-800 |
| Lift + Cruise | 300-600 |
| Jet VTOL | 2000-5000 |

Lower disk loading = better hover efficiency

#### 2.2 Download in Hover

Wing download:
D_wing = C_D_wing × q_induced × S_wing

Where q_induced is from rotor wake.

Typical download:
- Tiltrotor: 10-15% of thrust
- Tiltwing: 5-10% of thrust
- Quadplane: 5-15% (depends on geometry)

Download mitigation:
- Flap deflection in hover
- Wing positioning
- Nacelle design

#### 2.3 Ground Effect

Thrust increase in ground effect:
T_IGE/T_OGE = 1 + (R/(4×h))²

For h < R, significant benefit.

VTOL benefits from reduced power in ground effect.

### 3. Transition Aerodynamics

#### 3.1 Transition Corridor

Safe transition requires:
- Sufficient airspeed for wing lift
- Adequate control authority
- Stable flight path

Minimum transition speed:
V_min ≈ √(2×W/(ρ×S×C_L_max)) × 0.8

#### 3.2 Wing Stall During Transition

During slow transition:
- High angle of attack
- Possible flow separation
- Rotor wake affects wing

Design solutions:
- Leading edge slats
- Blown flaps
- Proper transition scheduling

#### 3.3 Control During Transition

Blended control:
- Low speed: Rotor/thrust vector control
- High speed: Aerodynamic surfaces
- Transition: Both systems active

Control allocation:
Smooth handoff between systems.

### 4. Tiltrotor Specific Design

#### 4.1 Rotor/Propeller Design

Compromise between:
- Hover efficiency (large diameter, low disk loading)
- Cruise efficiency (small diameter, high advance ratio)
- Structural weight

Typical compromise:
- Disk loading: 500-800 N/m²
- Blade number: 3-4
- Solidity: 0.1-0.15

#### 4.2 Conversion Mechanism

Tilt angle range: 0° (cruise) to 90°+ (hover)
Tilt time: 10-20 seconds typical

Nacelle structural requirements:
- Support rotor loads in all orientations
- Withstand transition aerodynamic loads
- House drive system

#### 4.3 Wing Design

Short, thick wing:
- High wing loading acceptable
- Rotor downwash impingement
- Structural depth for loads

Aeroelastic considerations:
- Whirl flutter
- Wing/rotor coupling
- Requires careful frequency placement

### 5. Lift + Cruise Configuration

#### 5.1 Lift Rotor Sizing

Hover thrust requirement:
T_total = W × 1.1 (10% margin)

Per rotor (N rotors):
T_rotor = T_total / N

Rotor area:
A = T / (2 × DL_target)

Power per rotor:
P = T^1.5 / (FM × √(2×ρ×A))

#### 5.2 Cruise Propulsion Sizing

Cruise drag:
D = 0.5 × ρ × V² × S × C_D

Required cruise thrust = D

Propeller/motor sizing for cruise power:
P_cruise = D × V / η_prop

#### 5.3 Energy Considerations

Hover energy:
E_hover = P_hover × t_hover

Cruise energy:
E_cruise = P_cruise × t_cruise

Total energy:
E_total = E_hover + E_cruise + E_transition + E_reserve

Battery sizing:
m_battery = E_total / (η × specific_energy)

### 6. Quadplane Design

#### 6.1 Configuration Layout

Rotor placement options:
- Quad: 4 rotors at corners
- Hex: 6 rotors for redundancy
- Octo: 8 rotors for heavy lift

Wing integration:
- Above fuselage: Clean wing, complex structure
- Through fuselage: Structural efficiency
- Below fuselage: CG considerations

#### 6.2 Transition Strategy

Gradual transition:
1. Hover with quad rotors
2. Tilt forward, build airspeed
3. Wing generates lift
4. Reduce rotor thrust
5. Full wing-borne flight

Back-transition:
1. Slow to near stall
2. Increase rotor thrust
3. Pitch up to reduce speed
4. Full rotor-borne hover

#### 6.3 Rotor Management in Cruise

Options:
- Rotors continue spinning (drag penalty)
- Rotors stopped (folding optional)
- Rotors feathered (reduced drag)

Folding props:
- Reduces cruise drag by 30-50%
- Mechanical complexity
- Restart reliability

### 7. Control Systems

#### 7.1 Hover Control

Using differential thrust:
- Pitch: Front/rear thrust difference
- Roll: Left/right thrust difference
- Yaw: Clockwise/counter-clockwise difference
- Heave: Total thrust change

Control allocation matrix:
[T1]   [1  1  1  1]   [Thrust]
[T2] = [1 -1 -1  1] × [Roll  ]
[T3]   [1 -1  1 -1]   [Pitch ]
[T4]   [1  1 -1 -1]   [Yaw   ]

#### 7.2 Transition Control

Blending function:
α_rotor = f(airspeed)
α_surface = 1 - α_rotor

Control input:
u_actual = α_rotor × u_rotor + α_surface × u_surface

#### 7.3 Cruise Control

Conventional aircraft control:
- Elevator: Pitch
- Ailerons: Roll  
- Rudder: Yaw
- Throttle: Speed

Optional rotor assist:
- Tight turns
- Slow flight
- Emergency

### 8. eVTOL Considerations

#### 8.1 Electric Propulsion Benefits

For VTOL:
- Distributed propulsion easy
- Instant torque response
- Redundancy through multiple motors
- Quiet operation

DEP (Distributed Electric Propulsion):
- Many small rotors
- Blown lift for transition
- Reduced tip speed, lower noise

#### 8.2 Battery Limitations

Energy density:
- Current Li-ion: 250 Wh/kg
- Near-term: 400 Wh/kg
- Required for practical eVTOL: >500 Wh/kg

Power density:
- Peak power demands in hover
- Battery must supply high C-rate

#### 8.3 Range/Payload Tradeoffs

Simplified range equation:
R = E_battery × η / (P_cruise/V)
R = (m_battery × e_specific × η × V) / P_cruise

Payload sensitivity:
dR/dW_payload = -R × (1/(W) + 1.5×P_hover/(P_cruise×W))

### 9. Design Example: eVTOL Air Taxi

#### 9.1 Requirements
- Passengers: 4 + pilot
- Range: 60 km
- Cruise speed: 200 km/h
- Hover time: 2 minutes

#### 9.2 Weight Estimate
- Empty weight: 800 kg
- Passengers: 400 kg
- Battery: 400 kg
- MTOW: 1,600 kg

#### 9.3 Propulsion Sizing

Hover disk loading: 400 N/m²
Total disk area: 39.2 m² (8 rotors, 2.5m diameter each)

Hover power (FM=0.7):
P_hover = 350 kW

Cruise power:
P_cruise = 120 kW

Battery energy (400 Wh/kg):
E_battery = 160 kWh

Range check:
R = (160 kWh × 0.85 × 200 km/h) / 120 kW = 227 km ✓
