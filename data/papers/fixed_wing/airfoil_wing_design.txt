# Airfoil Selection and Wing Design
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Airfoil Geometry

#### 1.1 Airfoil Parameters

Chord line: Straight line from leading edge to trailing edge
Camber line: Mean line between upper and lower surfaces
Maximum camber: Greatest distance between camber line and chord
Maximum thickness: Greatest distance between upper and lower surfaces

Key dimensions:
- Chord (c): Reference length
- Maximum thickness (t): Usually 8-18% of chord
- Maximum camber (m): Usually 0-6% of chord
- Position of max thickness: Usually 25-40% chord
- Position of max camber: Usually 30-50% chord

#### 1.2 NACA Airfoil Nomenclature

4-digit series (e.g., NACA 2412):
- First digit: Max camber (% chord) = 2%
- Second digit: Position of max camber (tenths chord) = 40%
- Last two digits: Max thickness (% chord) = 12%

5-digit series (e.g., NACA 23012):
- First digit: Design lift coefficient = 3/20 × 2 = 0.3
- Second digit: Position of max camber (% chord) = 15%
- Third digit: Camber type (0 = standard)
- Last two digits: Thickness = 12%

6-series (e.g., NACA 65₃-418):
- 6: Series designation
- 5: Position of min pressure (tenths chord) = 50%
- 3: Range of CL for low drag (tenths)
- 4: Design CL (tenths) = 0.4
- 18: Thickness = 18%

### 2. Airfoil Aerodynamics

#### 2.1 Lift Coefficient

Thin airfoil theory:
CL = 2π(α - α_L0)

Where α_L0 is zero-lift angle.

For cambered airfoils:
α_L0 ≈ -2 × (max camber in %)°

Lift curve slope:
CL_α = dCL/dα ≈ 2π per radian ≈ 0.11 per degree

Real airfoils: CL_α ≈ 0.10-0.11 per degree

#### 2.2 Maximum Lift

CL_max depends on:
- Airfoil shape
- Reynolds number
- Surface roughness
- Mach number

Typical values:
- Thin symmetric: CL_max = 0.8-1.0
- General aviation: CL_max = 1.4-1.6
- High-lift: CL_max = 1.8-2.2
- With flaps: CL_max = 2.5-3.5

#### 2.3 Drag Coefficient

Total drag coefficient:
CD = CD_min + CD_L

CD_min = CD at zero lift (profile drag)
CD_L = Drag due to lift = k × CL²

Drag polar:
CD = CD_0 + CL² / (π × e × AR)

Typical CD_min values:
- Laminar airfoil: 0.004-0.008
- Turbulent airfoil: 0.008-0.012
- Thick airfoil: 0.012-0.020

#### 2.4 Pitching Moment

Pitching moment coefficient about quarter chord:
Cm_c/4 ≈ -π/2 × (max camber in %) × (position/50%)

For symmetric airfoil: Cm_c/4 = 0

Moment about aerodynamic center:
Cm_ac = constant (independent of α)

### 3. Reynolds Number Effects

#### 3.1 Reynolds Number Definition

Re = ρ × V × c / μ = V × c / ν

Typical values:
- Model aircraft: Re = 10⁵ - 10⁶
- UAV: Re = 10⁵ - 5×10⁶
- General aviation: Re = 3×10⁶ - 10⁷
- Transport: Re = 10⁷ - 5×10⁷

#### 3.2 Low Reynolds Number Effects

At Re < 5×10⁵:
- Laminar separation bubbles
- Reduced CL_max
- Increased drag
- Hysteresis effects

Design considerations:
- Use airfoils designed for low Re
- Consider turbulators
- Avoid very thick sections

#### 3.3 High Reynolds Number

At Re > 10⁷:
- Mostly turbulent boundary layer
- Higher skin friction
- More predictable behavior
- Less sensitive to roughness

### 4. Airfoil Selection Guidelines

#### 4.1 By Application

Gliders:
- Low drag paramount
- Wortmann FX, Eppler E
- 12-16% thickness
- 2-4% camber

General aviation:
- NACA 4-digit, 5-digit
- 12-15% thickness
- 2-4% camber
- Good stall characteristics

Aerobatic:
- Symmetric or low camber
- NACA 0009-0012
- Quick response

High lift (STOL):
- Thick sections (15-18%)
- High camber with flaps
- Slats for leading edge

Racing:
- Laminar flow sections
- Low drag at design CL
- Thin (10-12%)

#### 4.2 By Speed Regime

Low speed (< 50 m/s):
- Thick sections OK
- High camber beneficial
- Consider Re effects

Medium speed (50-150 m/s):
- Balance L/D and structure
- 12-15% thickness typical

High subsonic (M > 0.6):
- Supercritical sections
- Reduced thickness (10-12%)
- Delayed drag rise

### 5. Three-Dimensional Wing Effects

#### 5.1 Finite Wing Lift

CL_wing = CL_2D × (AR / (AR + 2))

For high AR wings:
CL_wing ≈ CL_2D × (1 - 2/AR)

Lift curve slope:
a = a_0 / (1 + a_0/(π × e × AR))

Where a_0 ≈ 2π.

#### 5.2 Induced Drag

CD_i = CL² / (π × e × AR)

Oswald efficiency factor:
e = 1.78 × (1 - 0.045 × AR^0.68) - 0.64

Typical values: e = 0.75-0.85

Span efficiency:
e = 1 for elliptical lift distribution.

#### 5.3 Wingtip Effects

Tip vortex reduces effective angle of attack:
α_i = CL / (π × AR)

Induced drag:
Di = L × α_i = L × CL / (π × AR)

Winglets reduce induced drag by 5-15%.

### 6. High-Lift Devices

#### 6.1 Trailing Edge Flaps

Plain flap:
ΔCL_max ≈ 0.9 × CL_α × δ_f × S_f/S

Slotted flap:
ΔCL_max ≈ 1.3 × plain flap

Fowler flap:
ΔCL_max ≈ 1.3 × slotted + area increase

Typical ΔCL_max:
- Plain flap: 0.7-0.9
- Single slotted: 0.9-1.2
- Double slotted: 1.2-1.6
- Triple slotted: 1.5-1.9

#### 6.2 Leading Edge Devices

Slats:
ΔCL_max ≈ 0.4-0.6
Delay stall to higher α

Krueger flaps:
ΔCL_max ≈ 0.3-0.5

Leading edge droop:
ΔCL_max ≈ 0.2-0.3

#### 6.3 Combined Systems

Total CL_max = CL_max_clean + ΔCL_LE + ΔCL_TE

Modern transport:
CL_max (takeoff) ≈ 2.5-3.0
CL_max (landing) ≈ 3.0-3.5

### 7. Wing Planform Design

#### 7.1 Aspect Ratio Selection

High AR benefits:
- Lower induced drag
- Better L/D

High AR penalties:
- Heavier structure
- Lower roll rate
- Flutter concerns

Typical AR:
- Sailplanes: 20-40
- GA: 6-10
- Transport: 8-12
- Fighter: 3-5

#### 7.2 Taper Ratio

λ = c_tip / c_root

Elliptical loading approximation:
λ_opt ≈ 0.4 for unswept wings

Typical values:
- Rectangular: λ = 1.0
- Moderate taper: λ = 0.4-0.6
- High taper: λ = 0.2-0.4

#### 7.3 Twist (Washout)

Geometric twist:
ε = α_root - α_tip

Typical washout: 2-4°

Benefits:
- Delays tip stall
- Improves stall characteristics
- Reduces induced drag (with proper design)

### 8. Common Airfoil Data

#### 8.1 NACA 2412
- CL_max: 1.5
- α_stall: 16°
- CL_α: 0.105/deg
- CD_min: 0.0065
- Cm_ac: -0.05

#### 8.2 Clark-Y
- CL_max: 1.4
- α_stall: 14°
- Popular for model aircraft
- Flat bottom

#### 8.3 Eppler 387
- CL_max: 1.2
- Low Reynolds number design
- Good for UAVs
- Re = 2×10⁵
