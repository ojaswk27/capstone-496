# Glider Structural Design
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Design Load Cases

#### 1.1 Flight Envelope

Limit load factors (Utility):
- Positive: +5.3g at V_A
- Negative: -2.65g at V_A
- Positive: +4.0g at V_D
- Negative: -1.5g at V_D

Ultimate load: 1.5 × Limit load

Reference speeds:
- V_S: Stall speed (1g)
- V_A: Maneuvering speed
- V_NE: Never exceed speed
- V_W: Winch launch speed

#### 1.2 Gust Loads

Sharp-edge gust formula:
Δn = (K_g × ρ × V × U_de × a)/(2 × W/S)

Where:
- K_g = Gust alleviation factor
- U_de = Derived gust velocity (±15 m/s at V_C)
- a = Lift curve slope

Gust critical cases:
- Maximum gust at V_C
- Maximum gust at V_D (reduced gust)

#### 1.3 Ground Loads

Winch launch:
- Vertical: 4-5g
- Horizontal: 2-3g forward
- Asymmetric side loads

Aerotow:
- Lower loads than winch
- Towline failure case

Landing:
- 2-3g vertical at touchdown
- Side load for crosswind

### 2. Wing Structure

#### 2.1 Spar Design

Main spar carries:
- Bending moment (caps)
- Shear (web)

Bending moment distribution:
M(y) = L_total × y × (1 - 2y/b)²/4 (approximate for elliptical)

Spar cap stress:
σ = M × h/2 / I

Cap material typically:
- Carbon fiber unidirectional
- Tensile strength: 1500+ MPa
- Design allowable: 500-800 MPa

#### 2.2 Wing Skin

Functions:
- Aerodynamic surface
- Shear load path
- Torsion resistance

Construction:
- Fiberglass ±45° layers (most gliders)
- Carbon for high-performance
- Sandwich with foam or honeycomb core

Minimum thickness:
- Leading edge: 1-2 mm fiberglass
- Mid-chord: Sandwich, 10-20 mm total
- Trailing edge: 0.5-1 mm

#### 2.3 Torsion Box

Closed section for torsion:
τ = T / (2 × A_enclosed × t)

Shear flow:
q = T / (2 × A_enclosed)

D-box construction:
- Leading edge to spar
- Skin carries torsion
- Efficient for composite wings

#### 2.4 Wing-Fuselage Connection

Types:
- Spar carry-through (single piece)
- Wing root fittings (detachable)

Fitting loads:
- Vertical shear: Full lift
- Fore-aft: Drag + pitching moment
- Torsion: Wing twisting loads

Typical hardware:
- Steel or titanium pins
- 4140 or 4340 steel common
- Bushing for wear

### 3. Fuselage Structure

#### 3.1 Loads

Bending:
- Tail loads
- Landing gear loads
- Inertia loads

Torsion:
- Asymmetric tail loads
- Aileron-rudder coupling

Concentrated loads:
- Wing attachment
- Landing gear
- Cockpit/canopy

#### 3.2 Construction Types

Monocoque:
- Shell carries all loads
- Simple for composites
- Common for gliders

Semi-monocoque:
- Shell + frames/bulkheads
- Concentrated load introduction

Typical materials:
- Fiberglass/epoxy skin
- Aramid (Kevlar) in impact areas
- Carbon for stiffness

#### 3.3 Cockpit Area

Requirements:
- Crashworthiness (15-20g forward)
- Visibility
- Ergonomics

Canopy:
- Acrylic or polycarbonate
- Jettison capability
- Sealing for aerodynamics

### 4. Tail Structure

#### 4.1 Horizontal Tail

Design loads:
- Balancing loads (trim)
- Maneuvering loads
- Gust loads

All-moving (stabilator):
- Common on high-performance
- Less drag
- More complex actuation

Fixed stabilizer + elevator:
- Simpler
- Training gliders

#### 4.2 Vertical Tail

Design loads:
- Sideslip: β_max at V_A
- Rudder kicks
- Spin recovery

T-tail considerations:
- Flutter (tail-fuselage)
- Deep stall possibility
- Higher loads on vertical

#### 4.3 Control Surfaces

Hinge moments:
H = q × S × c × C_h

Where C_h = Hinge moment coefficient

Mass balance:
- Prevent flutter
- Weight forward of hinge
- Static and dynamic balance

### 5. Landing Gear

#### 5.1 Main Wheel

Location:
- Near CG for ground handling
- Retractable on high-performance

Shock absorption:
- Rubber donuts
- Oleo (rare on gliders)
- Tire deflection

Design loads:
- Sink rate: 0.5-1.0 m/s typical
- Load factor: 2-3g at wheel

#### 5.2 Tail/Nose Wheel

Tailwheel (conventional):
- Simple, light
- Most gliders
- Ground loop tendency

Nose wheel:
- Better ground handling
- Heavier
- Some training gliders

#### 5.3 Wing Tip Protection

Tip skids/wheels:
- Ground contact during taxi
- Protect wing tip
- Replaceable wear items

### 6. Material Selection

#### 6.1 Composite Properties

E-glass/epoxy:
- Density: 1.9 g/cm³
- Tensile strength: 500-700 MPa
- Modulus: 25-35 GPa
- Good impact resistance

Carbon/epoxy (UD):
- Density: 1.55 g/cm³
- Tensile strength: 1500-2000 MPa
- Modulus: 130-150 GPa
- Brittle in impact

Aramid (Kevlar)/epoxy:
- Density: 1.4 g/cm³
- Tensile strength: 1200-1400 MPa
- Excellent impact resistance
- Difficult to machine

#### 6.2 Core Materials

PVC foam (Divinycell):
- Density: 40-200 kg/m³
- Shear strength: 0.5-3.5 MPa
- Good fatigue properties

Honeycomb (Nomex):
- Density: 30-130 kg/m³
- Higher shear strength
- More expensive

Styrofoam:
- Low cost
- Lower properties
- Used in wing cores

#### 6.3 Metal Components

Steel (4130, 4340):
- Landing gear
- Fittings
- Control system parts

Aluminum (2024, 6061):
- Non-structural items
- Some fittings
- Control pushrods

Titanium:
- High-load fittings
- Weight critical areas

### 7. Manufacturing Considerations

#### 7.1 Composite Layup

Hand layup:
- Most common
- Labor intensive
- Variable quality

Prepreg:
- Consistent properties
- Higher cost
- Requires autoclave/oven

Infusion:
- Good fiber/resin ratio
- Complex setup
- Growing use

#### 7.2 Tolerances

Aerodynamic surfaces:
- Profile: ±0.5 mm
- Waviness: <0.5 mm/100 mm
- Joints: Flush ±0.2 mm

Structural:
- Spar alignment: ±1 mm
- Hole location: ±0.1 mm
- Bushing fit: H7/g6
