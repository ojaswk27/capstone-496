# Thermal Soaring and Cross-Country Techniques
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Thermal Formation

#### 1.1 Thermal Sources

Surface heating:
- Dark surfaces (roads, plowed fields)
- Dry soil vs. vegetated areas
- Urban heat islands
- Rocky terrain, cliffs

Temperature differential required:
ΔT_surface = 5-10°C above ambient for thermal formation

Thermal trigger:
- Lift-off when convective instability reached
- Often at terrain features (ridges, edges)

#### 1.2 Thermal Structure

Core:
- Strongest lift at center
- Typical diameter: 100-500 m
- Lift: 2-6 m/s typical

Ring structure:
- Lift decreases radially
- May have sink around edges

Thermal height:
- Convective boundary layer top
- Marked by cumulus cloud base
- 1000-3000 m typical in temperate regions

#### 1.3 Atmospheric Conditions

Lapse rate:
- Dry adiabatic: 9.8°C/km
- Environmental lapse rate > 6°C/km for good thermals
- Inversion caps thermal development

Trigger temperature:
T_trigger = T_surface - (lapse_rate × mixing_height)

Best soaring conditions:
- Solar heating (10 AM - 5 PM)
- Low humidity
- Light winds (<15 kt)
- No inversions below 2000 m

### 2. Centering Thermals

#### 2.1 Entry Techniques

Random entry:
- Note heading when lift encountered
- Complete 360° turn
- Note where lift was strongest
- Shift circle toward strong lift

Systematic search:
- Fly through core initially
- Mark position of max lift
- Turn back to center

#### 2.2 Centering Methods

Visual references:
- Note horizon position in lift
- Shift circle toward that reference

Vario-based:
- Listen for increasing tone
- Steepen bank as lift increases
- Flatten when lift decreases

GPS-assisted (modern varios):
- Thermal core location estimated
- Display shows direction to center

#### 2.3 Circling Performance

Circle radius:
R = V² / (g × tan(φ))

Sink rate in turn:
V_s_turn = V_s_straight / cos³(φ)

Load factor:
n = 1 / cos(φ)

Optimal bank angle trade-off:
- Steeper bank → smaller radius → more time in core
- Steeper bank → higher sink rate

Typical thermal circling:
- Bank angle: 30-45°
- Speed: 1.3-1.5 × V_stall
- Radius: 100-200 m

### 3. Speed to Fly Theory

#### 3.1 MacCready Theory

Optimal inter-thermal speed:
Fly faster when:
- Strong thermals expected
- Time is valuable
- High on glide

MacCready ring setting:
- Set to expected climb rate in next thermal
- Read optimal speed from polar

Speed polar shift:
V_opt increases by √(1 + MC/V_s_min)

#### 3.2 Dolphin Flying

In rising air: Slow down
In sinking air: Speed up

Speed adjustment:
ΔV = k × w

Where:
- w = Vertical air mass motion
- k = Sensitivity factor (1-2 typical)

Benefits:
- Extract energy from lift without circling
- Faster average cross-country speed
- Works best in wave, convergence, weak thermals

#### 3.3 Final Glide

Calculate arrival height:
h_arrival = h_current - D/L/D_eff

Effective L/D accounts for:
- Headwind: (L/D)_eff = (L/D) × V_TAS/(V_TAS + V_wind)
- Sink rate: Include time spent in sink

Safety margin:
- Pattern altitude + 300 m minimum
- Account for possible sink areas

### 4. Cross-Country Navigation

#### 4.1 Turnpoint Selection

Optimal turnpoint factors:
- Good thermal sources nearby
- Landable fields available
- Clear of airspace restrictions

Task types:
- Racing task: Fixed turnpoints
- Assigned Area Task (AAT): Variable radius
- Free distance: Maximize distance

#### 4.2 Route Selection

Cloud street:
- Follow line of cumulus
- Reduced circling time
- May allow dolphin flying

Convergence zone:
- Wind boundaries
- Sea breeze fronts
- Mountain/valley flows

Energy lines:
- Connect thermal sources
- Stay over good terrain

#### 4.3 Start and Finish

Start timing:
- Start when thermals fully developed
- Consider traffic at popular start times

Finish technique:
- Final glide from distance
- Maximize finish height-speed trade-off
- Account for terrain and sink

### 5. Other Lift Sources

#### 5.1 Ridge Lift

Wind deflected by terrain:
- Lift extends 2-3× ridge height
- Best when wind perpendicular to ridge
- Wind speed: 15-25 kt optimal

Lift estimate:
w ≈ V_wind × sin(slope_angle)

Ridge soaring rules:
- Keep ridge on left (in right-hand pattern)
- Pass oncoming traffic on ridge side
- Turn away from ridge

#### 5.2 Wave Lift

Mountain wave characteristics:
- Smooth, laminar lift
- Can extend to extreme altitudes (40,000+ ft)
- Rotor turbulence below
- Lenticular clouds as indicator

Wave wavelength:
λ = 2π × V_wind / N

Where N is Brunt-Väisälä frequency

Accessing wave:
- Launch into rotor
- Climb in thermal/rotor
- Transition to laminar wave

#### 5.3 Convergence Lift

Sea breeze front:
- Interface of air masses
- Linear lift source
- Can travel with front

Outflow boundaries:
- Thunderstorm gust fronts
- Caution: Severe weather nearby

### 6. Performance Measurement

#### 6.1 Average Speed

Cross-country average speed:
V_avg = Distance / Time

For thermalling flight:
V_avg = V_cruise × R_climb / (R_climb + V_sink_cruise)

Where R_climb = Average climb rate

#### 6.2 Achieved L/D

Achieved glide ratio:
(L/D)_achieved = Distance / Altitude_lost

Accounts for:
- Air mass motion
- Pilot technique
- Weather conditions

#### 6.3 Performance Indices

Handicap system:
- Index based on glider performance
- Allows mixed-fleet competition
- 100 = Standard class reference

Speed index:
SI = (Distance × 100) / (Time × Handicap)

### 7. Safety Considerations

#### 7.1 Landout Planning

Always have field selected:
- Within glide range
- Suitable surface
- Clear approaches

Field selection criteria:
- Length > 400 m
- Into wind
- No obstructions
- Firm surface

Circuit planning:
- Fly standard pattern
- Plan for go-around margin
- Land in first third of field

#### 7.2 Weather Hazards

Overdevelopment:
- Watch cumulus growth
- Avoid if tops exceed freezing level
- Exit area before cu-nimbus develops

Spread-out:
- High cirrus reduces heating
- Thermals weaken and die
- Plan early return

Blue conditions:
- No cumulus markers
- Thermals still present but harder to find
- Lower climb rates typical
