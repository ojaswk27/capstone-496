# Glider Aerodynamics and Performance
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Glide Performance Fundamentals

#### 1.1 Glide Ratio

The glide ratio (L/D) is the fundamental measure of glider efficiency:
L/D = Lift/Drag = V_horizontal/V_vertical = Distance/Altitude_lost

For steady, unaccelerated glide:
L = W * cos(γ)
D = W * sin(γ)

Where γ is the glide angle.

Glide ratio:
L/D = cot(γ) = 1/tan(γ)

For small angles: L/D ≈ 1/γ (γ in radians)

Typical values:
- Training glider: 25-30:1
- Standard class: 38-42:1
- Open class: 50-60:1
- Albatross (bird): 20:1

#### 1.2 Sink Rate

Sink rate (rate of descent):
V_s = V * sin(γ) = V / (L/D)

Also expressed as:
V_s = D * V / W = (C_D / C_L) * √(2W / (ρ * S * C_L))

Minimum sink rate occurs at:
C_L = √(3 * C_D0 * π * e * AR)

V_min_sink = √(2W / (ρ * S)) * (4 * k / (3 * C_D0))^(1/4)

Where k = 1/(π * e * AR)

#### 1.3 Speed Polar

The speed polar describes sink rate vs airspeed:
V_s = V / (L/D) = f(V)

Simplified form:
V_s = a * V³ + b / V

Where:
- a = ρ * S * C_D0 / (2 * W) (parasite drag contribution)
- b = 2 * k * W / (ρ * S) (induced drag contribution)

### 2. Optimal Flight Speeds

#### 2.1 Minimum Sink Speed

Speed for minimum sink rate:
V_ms = √(2W / (ρ * S)) * (k / (3 * C_D0))^(1/4)

Minimum sink speed gives maximum time aloft in still air.

#### 2.2 Best Glide Speed (Max L/D)

Speed for maximum L/D:
V_bg = √(2W / (ρ * S)) * (k / C_D0)^(1/4)

Relationship:
V_bg = V_ms * 3^(1/4) ≈ 1.316 * V_ms

Best glide speed gives maximum distance in still air.

#### 2.3 Speed to Fly (MacCready Theory)

In rising or sinking air, optimal speed changes:

McCready ring setting:
V_opt = f(V_mc)

Where V_mc is the expected climb rate in the next thermal.

Speed to fly in sinking air (-w):
V_fly = V_bg * √(1 + w/V_s_min)

Simplified rule: Add 5-10 knots for each knot of sink.

### 3. Thermal Soaring

#### 3.1 Thermal Characteristics

Thermal updraft velocity:
w = w_core * exp(-(r/R)²)

Where:
- w_core = Core lift (typically 2-5 m/s)
- r = Distance from center
- R = Thermal radius (100-500 m)

Thermal strength varies with:
- Time of day (peaks 2-4 PM)
- Surface heating
- Lapse rate
- Humidity

#### 3.2 Circling Performance

Climb rate in thermal:
V_climb = w_thermal - V_sink_circling

Sink rate while circling:
V_s_circle = V_s_straight / cos³(φ)

Where φ is bank angle.

Load factor:
n = 1/cos(φ)

Circling radius:
R = V² / (g * tan(φ))

Optimal bank angle balances:
- Tighter circle = more time in core
- Steeper bank = higher sink rate

Typical bank angles: 30-45°

#### 3.3 Average Cross-Country Speed

Simplified average speed:
V_avg = (V_cruise * V_climb) / (V_climb + V_sink_cruise)

Optimal cruise speed increases with:
- Stronger thermals
- Higher glide ratio

### 4. Wing Design

#### 4.1 Aspect Ratio

High aspect ratio essential for efficiency:
L/D_max ∝ √(AR / C_D0)

Typical aspect ratios:
- Training gliders: 15-18
- Standard class: 20-24
- Open class: 25-35

Structural challenges:
- Higher AR = longer span = more bending moment
- Flutter considerations
- Weight penalty

#### 4.2 Wing Airfoils

Glider airfoils optimized for:
- Low drag at design C_L
- Good performance over C_L range
- Acceptable stall characteristics

Common glider airfoils:
- Wortmann FX series
- Eppler E series
- HQ (Horstmann-Quast) series

Laminar flow extent:
- Upper surface: 50-70% chord
- Lower surface: 30-50% chord

#### 4.3 Winglets and Span Extensions

Winglet benefits:
- Reduce induced drag
- Effective span increase
- Cosmetic span limit workaround

Span extension vs winglet:
- Extension: ~3% L/D improvement per meter
- Winglet: ~1-2% L/D improvement

### 5. Performance Calculations

#### 5.1 Still Air Performance

Distance from altitude h:
D = h * (L/D)

Time aloft:
T = h / V_s

Cross-country potential:
R = h * L/D (theoretical maximum)

#### 5.2 Wind Effects

Headwind:
- Fly faster than best glide
- V_opt = V_bg + 0.5 * V_wind (approximate)

Tailwind:
- Fly slower than best glide
- V_opt = V_bg - 0.5 * V_wind (approximate)

Ground speed:
V_g = V_air ± V_wind

Effective L/D:
(L/D)_eff = V_g / V_s = (V_air ± V_wind) / V_s

#### 5.3 Altitude Effects

True airspeed at altitude:
TAS = EAS * √(ρ_SL / ρ_alt) = EAS * √(σ)

Sink rate at altitude:
V_s_alt = V_s_SL * √(σ)

Glide ratio is independent of altitude (for incompressible flow).

### 6. Water Ballast

#### 6.1 Ballast Effects

Wing loading with ballast:
W/S_ballast = W/S_empty + m_water/S

Speed increase:
V_ballast / V_empty = √(W_ballast / W_empty)

Sink rate increase:
V_s_ballast / V_s_empty = √(W_ballast / W_empty)

#### 6.2 When to Use Ballast

Use ballast when:
- Strong thermals expected
- Long glides between thermals
- Fast cruise desired

Dump ballast when:
- Weak thermals
- Landing
- Competition finish

#### 6.3 Ballast Capacity

Typical ballast:
- Standard class: 100-150 liters
- Open class: 200-300 liters

Maximum wing loading:
- Standard class: 45-55 kg/m²
- Open class: 55-65 kg/m²

### 7. Glider Specifications

#### 7.1 Training Glider (SGS 2-33)
- Span: 15.6 m
- Wing area: 20.0 m²
- Aspect ratio: 12.2
- Empty weight: 272 kg
- Max weight: 476 kg
- Wing loading: 23.8 kg/m²
- Best L/D: 23:1 at 72 km/h
- Min sink: 0.83 m/s at 61 km/h
- Stall speed: 52 km/h

#### 7.2 Standard Class (LS-8)
- Span: 15.0 m
- Wing area: 10.5 m²
- Aspect ratio: 21.4
- Empty weight: 245 kg
- Max weight: 525 kg
- Wing loading: 50 kg/m²
- Best L/D: 43:1 at 105 km/h
- Min sink: 0.57 m/s at 85 km/h

#### 7.3 Open Class (Eta)
- Span: 30.9 m
- Wing area: 18.7 m²
- Aspect ratio: 51
- Empty weight: 570 kg
- Max weight: 850 kg
- Wing loading: 45 kg/m²
- Best L/D: 70:1
- Min sink: 0.38 m/s

### 8. Safety Considerations

#### 8.1 Stall Characteristics
- Gentle stall preferred
- Wing drop tendency manageable
- Spin recovery straightforward

#### 8.2 Operating Limitations
- V_NE (Never Exceed): Marked on ASI
- V_A (Maneuvering): Full controls at this speed
- Maximum aero-tow speed
- Maximum winch launch speed

#### 8.3 Minimum Control Speeds
- Stall speed at 1g
- Minimum controllable speed
- Pattern speed: 1.3-1.5 * V_stall
