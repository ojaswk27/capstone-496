# Satellite Power and Thermal Systems
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Solar Array Design

#### 1.1 Solar Cell Performance

Solar cell power output:
P = η × S × A × cos(θ) × F_shadow × F_degradation

Where:
- η = Cell efficiency (0.20-0.32 for modern cells)
- S = Solar constant (1361 W/m² at 1 AU)
- A = Cell area (m²)
- θ = Incidence angle
- F_shadow = Shadowing factor (0-1)
- F_degradation = Degradation factor

Cell technologies:
- Silicon: 15-20% efficiency, lowest cost
- GaAs: 25-30% efficiency, radiation resistant
- Triple-junction: 28-32% efficiency, highest performance

#### 1.2 Degradation

Annual degradation rates:
- LEO: 2-3% per year
- GEO: 1-2% per year
- High radiation: 3-5% per year

End-of-life power:
P_EOL = P_BOL × (1 - d)^years

For 7-year LEO mission at 2.5%/year:
P_EOL = P_BOL × 0.833

#### 1.3 Array Sizing

Required array area:
A = P_required / (η × S × cos(θ_avg) × F_eclipse × F_deg)

Eclipse factor:
F_eclipse = T_sun / T_orbit

For LEO at 400 km (92 min orbit, 35 min eclipse):
F_eclipse = 57/92 = 0.62

#### 1.4 Array Configurations

Body-mounted panels:
- Simple, no deployment
- Limited area
- CubeSats, small satellites

Deployable wings:
- More area
- Single-axis tracking optional
- Medium to large satellites

Tracking arrays:
- Maximum power
- Complex mechanisms
- GEO satellites

### 2. Battery Systems

#### 2.1 Battery Types

Lithium-Ion:
- Energy density: 150-250 Wh/kg
- Cycle life: 500-2000 cycles (at 40% DOD)
- Voltage: 3.6-3.7 V/cell nominal
- Preferred for most missions

Lithium Polymer:
- Energy density: 180-250 Wh/kg
- Flexible form factor
- Common in CubeSats

Nickel-Hydrogen (legacy):
- Energy density: 50-60 Wh/kg
- Very long cycle life
- GEO satellites, ISS

#### 2.2 Battery Sizing

Battery capacity required:
C = P_eclipse × T_eclipse / (V_bus × DOD × η_battery)

Where:
- P_eclipse = Power during eclipse (W)
- T_eclipse = Eclipse duration (hours)
- DOD = Depth of discharge (0.2-0.4 typical)
- η_battery = Round-trip efficiency (0.85-0.95)

Example (LEO, 400 km):
P_eclipse = 50 W
T_eclipse = 0.58 hours
DOD = 0.3
η = 0.9
V_bus = 28 V

C = 50 × 0.58 / (28 × 0.3 × 0.9) = 3.8 Ah

#### 2.3 Cycle Life

Cycle life depends on DOD:
- 20% DOD: 10,000+ cycles
- 40% DOD: 2,000-5,000 cycles
- 60% DOD: 500-1,500 cycles

LEO cycles per year:
N_cycles = 365 × 24 / T_orbit_hours = 365 × 24 / 1.53 ≈ 5,700

### 3. Power Distribution

#### 3.1 Power Bus Architectures

Unregulated bus:
- Battery voltage varies with SOC
- Simple, lightweight
- 22-34 V for 28 V nominal

Regulated bus:
- Constant voltage output
- More complex
- Required for sensitive payloads

Typical bus voltages:
- Small satellites: 3.3, 5, 12 V
- Medium satellites: 28 V
- Large satellites: 50, 100 V

#### 3.2 Power Budget

Power margin requirements:
- Minimum: 10% margin
- Recommended: 20-30% margin
- Include contingency for anomalies

Sample power budget (3U CubeSat):
| Subsystem | Average (W) | Peak (W) |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| OBC | 0.5 | 1.0 |
| ADCS | 0.8 | 2.0 |
| Comm (Rx) | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| Comm (Tx) | 2.0 | 6.0 |
| Payload | 3.0 | 5.0 |
| Thermal | 0.5 | 2.0 |
| Margin (20%) | 1.5 | 3.3 |
| **Total** | **8.8** | **19.8** |

### 4. Thermal Control

#### 4.1 Thermal Environment

Heat sources:
- Solar radiation: 1361 W/m² (direct)
- Earth albedo: ~400 W/m² (30% of solar)
- Earth IR: ~240 W/m²
- Internal dissipation

Heat sinks:
- Radiation to space (2.7 K background)

#### 4.2 Thermal Balance Equation

Steady-state energy balance:
Q_in = Q_out

α_s × S × A_sun + α × S × F_a × A_earth + ε × σ × T_earth⁴ × F_e × A_earth + Q_internal = ε × σ × A_rad × T_sat⁴

Where:
- α_s = Solar absorptivity
- α = Albedo absorptivity  
- ε = Infrared emissivity
- F_a, F_e = View factors to Earth
- σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴

#### 4.3 Surface Coatings

Material properties (α/ε):
- White paint: 0.2/0.9
- Black paint: 0.95/0.9
- Bare aluminum: 0.15/0.05
- OSR (Optical Solar Reflector): 0.08/0.8
- Gold: 0.3/0.03
- MLI (effective): 0.01/0.01

Selecting α/ε ratio:
- High α/ε: Hot surface (sun-facing)
- Low α/ε: Cold surface (space-facing)
- α ≈ ε: Stable temperature

#### 4.4 Passive Thermal Control

Heat pipes:
- Effective thermal conductivity: 10,000+ W/mK
- Operating temperature range: -40 to +80°C
- Mass: 0.5-2 kg/m

Radiators:
- Area sizing: A = Q / (ε × σ × (T_hot⁴ - T_cold⁴))
- Fin effectiveness: 0.7-0.9
- Typical fin thickness: 1-3 mm

Multi-Layer Insulation (MLI):
- 10-20 layers typical
- Effective emissivity: 0.01-0.03
- Mass: 0.5-1.0 kg/m²

#### 4.5 Active Thermal Control

Heaters:
- Kapton film heaters
- Power density: 1-5 W/cm²
- Thermostat or software controlled

Louvers:
- Variable effective emissivity
- Passive or active actuation
- ε range: 0.1-0.6 typical

### 5. Temperature Requirements

#### 5.1 Component Limits

Typical operating ranges:
| Component | Min (°C) | Max (°C) |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Electronics | -20 | +60 |
| Batteries | 0 | +40 |
| Solar cells | -150 | +100 |
| Propellant | +10 | +40 |
| Optical sensors | -30 | +40 |
| Structure | -80 | +80 |

Survival (non-operating):
- Wider range than operating
- Typically ±20°C beyond operating

#### 5.2 Thermal Gradients

Maximum gradients:
- Electronics boards: 10-20°C
- Optical systems: 1-5°C
- Structure: 50-100°C acceptable

### 6. Power System Reliability

#### 6.1 Redundancy

Common approaches:
- N+1 battery strings
- Parallel solar cell strings
- Redundant power converters
- Fault isolation switches

Single-point failure avoidance:
- No single failure should lose >50% capability
- Critical loads have backup power paths

#### 6.2 Protection Features

Over-current protection:
- Fuses or current limiters
- Automatic reset preferred

Under-voltage lockout:
- Prevents deep discharge
- Battery disconnect at 2.7-2.8 V/cell

Over-temperature shutdown:
- Disconnect loads above limits
- Prevent thermal runaway
