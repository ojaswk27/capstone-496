# Satellite Launch and Deployment
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Launch Vehicle Selection

#### 1.1 Performance Requirements

Payload to orbit:
- Mass capability to target orbit
- Fairing volume constraints
- Vibration and acoustic environment

Launch site considerations:
- Latitude → Inclination efficiency
- Azimuth restrictions
- Coastal vs inland

Cost factors:
- $/kg to orbit
- Rideshare opportunities
- Dedicated vs shared launch

#### 1.2 Common Launch Vehicles

Small satellites:
- Rocket Lab Electron: 300 kg to LEO
- Firefly Alpha: 1,000 kg to LEO
- PSLV (India): 1,750 kg to LEO

Medium:
- SpaceX Falcon 9: 22,800 kg to LEO
- Ariane 62: 10,350 kg to LEO
- Soyuz: 7,000 kg to LEO

Heavy:
- SpaceX Falcon Heavy: 63,800 kg to LEO
- Ariane 64: 21,500 kg to LEO
- Delta IV Heavy: 28,790 kg to LEO

#### 1.3 Launch Costs (Approximate)

Per kg to LEO (2024):
- Falcon 9 (rideshare): $2,000-3,000
- Electron: $5,000-7,000
- Dedicated small: $10,000-20,000
- Traditional (Ariane, Soyuz): $10,000-15,000

CubeSat deployers:
- ISS deployment: $50,000-100,000 per U
- Rideshare: $25,000-50,000 per U

### 2. Launch Environment

#### 2.1 Acceleration Loads

Longitudinal:
- Liftoff: 1.5-3.0 g
- Max-Q: 3-5 g
- Stage burnout: 4-8 g

Lateral:
- Wind loads at liftoff: 0.5-2 g
- Steering/pogo: 0.5-1.5 g

Load factors for design:
- Limit load: 1.25 × maximum expected
- Ultimate: 1.5 × limit

#### 2.2 Vibration Environment

Random vibration:
- Overall: 8-15 g RMS
- Frequency range: 20-2000 Hz

Sine vibration:
- Pogo (longitudinal): 5-25 Hz, 1-2 g
- Lateral: 10-50 Hz, 0.5-1 g

Acoustic:
- Overall: 130-145 dB
- Peak at 200-500 Hz

#### 2.3 Shock Loads

Fairing separation:
- 2,000-5,000 g peak
- High frequency content

Satellite separation:
- 500-3,000 g
- Depends on separation system

Pyroshock response spectrum (SRS):
Design to 3× expected environment

#### 2.4 Thermal Environment

Pre-launch:
- Air conditioning maintained
- Temperature: 15-25°C

Ascent:
- Fairing heating (aero)
- Fairing venting (pressure)

Post-separation:
- Direct solar exposure
- Cold space exposure
- Rapid thermal transients

### 3. Separation Systems

#### 3.1 CubeSat Deployers

P-POD (3U):
- Spring deployment: 1-2 m/s
- Multiple satellites per deployer
- Standardized interface

ISIPOD (various sizes):
- Modular design
- 3U to 12U
- Door deployment

#### 3.2 Standard Separation Systems

Marman clamp:
- Ring around vehicle
- Pyrotechnic release
- Most common for larger sats

Lightband:
- Low-shock separation
- Spring deployment
- Smaller satellites

Motorized Lightband:
- Zero-shock release
- Multiple release capability
- Higher cost

#### 3.3 Separation Dynamics

Tip-off rate:
ω = M_offset / I × t_sep

Where:
- M_offset = CG offset moment
- t_sep = Separation time

Typical tip-off: <3°/s required

Separation velocity:
V_sep = F_spring × t / m

Typical: 0.5-2 m/s

Collision avoidance:
- Sufficient delta-V
- Proper orientation
- Tumble consideration

### 4. Orbit Insertion

#### 4.1 Direct Insertion

Launch vehicle delivers to final orbit:
- Simpler satellite design
- No onboard propulsion needed
- Less flexible

Common for:
- LEO circular orbits
- Sun-synchronous orbits
- Rideshare missions

#### 4.2 Transfer Orbit

GTO to GEO:
- Launch to GTO (perigee ~200 km, apogee ~35,786 km)
- Satellite provides apogee kick
- Multiple burns to circularize

Super-synchronous transfer:
- Apogee > GEO altitude
- Saves satellite propellant
- Longer transfer time

#### 4.3 Propulsion Requirements

LEO circularization (small):
Δv = 10-50 m/s (if needed)

GTO to GEO:
Δv ≈ 1,500-1,800 m/s

Inclination change (from 28° to 0°):
Δv ≈ 800 m/s (at GEO velocity)

### 5. Deployment Sequences

#### 5.1 Post-Separation Checklist

Immediate (seconds):
1. Separation detection
2. Safe/arm status
3. Initial stabilization (if needed)

Short-term (minutes):
4. Solar array deployment
5. Power system activation
6. Initial attitude acquisition

Medium-term (hours):
7. Antenna deployment
8. Payload activation
9. Ground contact

Long-term (days-weeks):
10. Orbit determination/correction
11. Attitude calibration
12. Payload checkout

#### 5.2 Deployable Systems

Solar arrays:
- Mechanism testing pre-launch
- Deployment confirmation sensing
- Latching for stability

Antennas:
- High-gain dish deployment
- Boom deployment
- RF testing post-deploy

Booms:
- Gravity gradient booms
- Magnetometer booms
- Inflatable structures

#### 5.3 Deployment Failures

Common issues:
- Incomplete deployment
- Deployment timing errors
- Thermal snap effects
- Binding or friction

Mitigation:
- Redundant release mechanisms
- Torque margin
- Ground testing
- Contingency procedures

### 6. Early Operations

#### 6.1 LEOP (Launch and Early Orbit Phase)

Duration: Hours to days

Activities:
- Health assessment
- Initial orbit determination
- Ground network checkout
- Preliminary payload test

Critical period:
- 24/7 operations typically
- Rapid anomaly response
- Limited telemetry initially

#### 6.2 Commissioning

Duration: Weeks to months

Activities:
- Full system checkout
- Calibration procedures
- Performance verification
- Operational readiness

Success criteria:
- All systems nominal
- Performance within spec
- Ready for operations

### 7. Ground Segment Interface

#### 7.1 Tracking Requirements

Initial acquisition:
- Predicted orbit elements
- Wide beam antenna
- Signal search

Tracking updates:
- Two-line elements (TLE)
- Precise orbit determination
- Conjunction assessment

#### 7.2 Command and Control

Uplink requirements:
- Authenticated commands
- Time-tagged operations
- Contingency commands

Telemetry requirements:
- Health monitoring
- Anomaly detection
- Trending analysis
