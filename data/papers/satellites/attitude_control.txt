# Satellite Attitude Determination and Control
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Attitude Representations

#### 1.1 Euler Angles

Roll-Pitch-Yaw (3-2-1 sequence):
R = R_z(ψ) × R_y(θ) × R_x(φ)

Rotation matrix:
R = [cos(ψ)cos(θ)  cos(ψ)sin(θ)sin(φ)-sin(ψ)cos(φ)  cos(ψ)sin(θ)cos(φ)+sin(ψ)sin(φ)]
    [sin(ψ)cos(θ)  sin(ψ)sin(θ)sin(φ)+cos(ψ)cos(φ)  sin(ψ)sin(θ)cos(φ)-cos(ψ)sin(φ)]
    [-sin(θ)       cos(θ)sin(φ)                       cos(θ)cos(φ)                    ]

Singularity at θ = ±90° (gimbal lock)

#### 1.2 Quaternions

Unit quaternion: q = [q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3]^T
Constraint: q_0² + q_1² + q_2² + q_3² = 1

Rotation matrix from quaternion:
R = [1-2(q_2²+q_3²)  2(q_1q_2-q_0q_3)  2(q_1q_3+q_0q_2)]
    [2(q_1q_2+q_0q_3)  1-2(q_1²+q_3²)  2(q_2q_3-q_0q_1)]
    [2(q_1q_3-q_0q_2)  2(q_2q_3+q_0q_1)  1-2(q_1²+q_2²)]

Quaternion multiplication:
q_1 ⊗ q_2 = [q_10q_20-q_1·q_2, q_10q_2+q_20q_1+q_1×q_2]^T

Kinematic equation:
q̇ = 0.5 × Ω(ω) × q

Where Ω is the quaternion rate matrix.

#### 1.3 Direction Cosine Matrix

Properties:
- R^T × R = I
- det(R) = 1
- R^(-1) = R^T

Kinematic equation:
Ṙ = R × [ω]×

Where [ω]× is the skew-symmetric matrix of angular velocity.

### 2. Attitude Sensors

#### 2.1 Sun Sensors

Coarse sun sensor (CSS):
- Accuracy: 1-5°
- FOV: Hemispherical
- Simple photodiodes

Fine sun sensor (FSS):
- Accuracy: 0.01-0.1°
- FOV: 30-60°
- More complex optics

Sun vector in body frame provides two-axis attitude information.

#### 2.2 Earth Horizon Sensors

Infrared horizon sensor:
- Accuracy: 0.1-1°
- Works in eclipse
- Provides nadir direction

Horizon crossing indicator:
- Simple, reliable
- Lower accuracy
- Used for spin-stabilized satellites

#### 2.3 Star Trackers

Accuracy: 1-10 arcseconds (0.0003-0.003°)
Update rate: 1-10 Hz
FOV: 10-20°

Three-axis attitude from single sensor.
Most accurate attitude sensor available.

Lost-in-space capability:
- Star catalog matching
- No a priori attitude required

#### 2.4 Magnetometers

Three-axis magnetic field measurement.
Accuracy: 0.5-2° (after calibration)
Requires magnetic field model (IGRF/WMM).

Provides two-axis information.
Best combined with other sensors.

#### 2.5 Gyroscopes

Rate measurement:
- MEMS: 0.1-10 °/hr drift
- FOG: 0.001-0.1 °/hr drift
- RLG: 0.0001-0.01 °/hr drift
- HRG: <0.001 °/hr drift

Integration for attitude:
θ(t) = θ(0) + ∫ω dt

Drift accumulation requires periodic updates.

### 3. Attitude Determination

#### 3.1 TRIAD Algorithm

Two vector measurements (e.g., sun and magnetic field):
v_1b, v_2b (body frame)
v_1i, v_2i (inertial frame)

Construct triads:
t_1 = v_1
t_2 = v_1 × v_2 / |v_1 × v_2|
t_3 = t_1 × t_2

Rotation matrix:
R = [t_1b t_2b t_3b] × [t_1i t_2i t_3i]^T

#### 3.2 QUEST Algorithm

Optimal single-frame attitude determination.
Minimize cost function:
J = Σ a_i × |v_ib - R × v_ii|²

Solution via quaternion eigenvalue problem.
More accurate than TRIAD.

#### 3.3 Extended Kalman Filter

State vector: x = [q, ω, b_gyro]^T

Prediction:
x_k+1 = f(x_k, u_k) + w_k

Update:
x_k = x_k- + K(z_k - h(x_k-))

Fuses gyro data with vector measurements.
Estimates gyro bias.
Standard approach for high-accuracy systems.

### 4. Attitude Control

#### 4.1 Disturbance Torques

Gravity gradient:
T_gg = (3μ/r³) × (n̂ × I × n̂)
Max for LEO: ~10⁻⁶ N·m (small sat)

Magnetic:
T_mag = m × B
m = residual magnetic moment
Typical: 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ N·m

Aerodynamic (LEO):
T_aero = 0.5 × ρ × V² × C_D × A × (r_cp - r_cm)
Altitude dependent: 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁴ N·m

Solar radiation pressure:
T_srp = (S/c) × A × (r_cp - r_cm) × cos(θ)
Typical: 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁵ N·m

#### 4.2 Reaction Wheels

Torque: τ = I_w × ω̇_w
Momentum: H = I_w × ω_w

Wheel sizing:
H_max > T_disturbance × T_orbit / 4

Typical specifications:
- Momentum: 0.1-50 N·m·s
- Torque: 0.01-0.5 N·m
- Power: 1-50 W

Configuration:
- 3 wheels: No redundancy
- 4 wheels: Pyramid configuration
- 4+ wheels: Full redundancy

Momentum management required (magnetic torquers, thrusters).

#### 4.3 Control Moment Gyros

Single gimbal CMG:
τ = H × ω_gimbal

Output torque >> input torque.
Used for large satellites, space stations.
Singularity management required.

#### 4.4 Magnetic Torquers

Torque: τ = m × B
m = N × I × A (magnetic dipole moment)

Limitations:
- Only perpendicular to B field
- Weak near equator (B parallel to orbit)
- Combined with other actuators

Sizing:
m_max > T_max / B_min

Typical: 0.1-10 A·m² for small sats

#### 4.5 Thrusters

Torque: τ = F × r (moment arm)

Advantages:
- Works in any orientation
- High torque available

Disadvantages:
- Consumables required
- Complex plumbing
- Coupling with translation

Used for:
- Slew maneuvers
- Momentum dumping
- Large angle pointing

### 5. Control System Design

#### 5.1 PD Control

τ_cmd = -K_p × q_error - K_d × ω

Quaternion error:
q_error = q_cmd^(-1) ⊗ q_actual

Typical gains:
K_p = 4 × I × ω_n²
K_d = 4 × I × ζ × ω_n

Where:
- ω_n = Natural frequency
- ζ = Damping ratio (0.7-1.0)

#### 5.2 Momentum Management

Wheel speed limiting:
|ω_w| < ω_max

Desaturation control:
τ_desat = K × H_w

Using magnetic torquers:
m_cmd = K × (B × H_w) / |B|²

Null-space control for >3 wheels.

### 6. Pointing Requirements

#### 6.1 Typical Accuracies

| Application | Pointing | Knowledge | Stability |
|-------------|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Earth observation | 0.1-1° | 0.01-0.1° | 0.01°/s |
| Communications | 0.1-0.5° | 0.05° | 0.001°/s |
| Science | 0.01° | 0.001° | 0.0001°/s |
| Astronomy | 1" | 0.1" | 0.01"/s |

#### 6.2 Jitter Requirements

High-resolution imaging:
- Jitter < 0.1 pixel during integration
- For 1 μrad IFOV, 1 ms integration: < 0.1 μrad/s

Vibration isolation often required.
