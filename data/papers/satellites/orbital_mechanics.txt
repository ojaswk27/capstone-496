# Satellite Orbital Mechanics and Design
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Orbital Mechanics Fundamentals

#### 1.1 Kepler's Laws

First Law: Orbits are ellipses with the central body at one focus.

Second Law: Equal areas swept in equal times.
dA/dt = h/2 = constant

Third Law: Period relationship.
T² = (4π²/μ) * a³

Where:
- μ = GM (gravitational parameter)
- For Earth: μ = 3.986004418 × 10¹⁴ m³/s²
- a = Semi-major axis

#### 1.2 Circular Orbit Velocity

Orbital velocity:
v = √(μ/r)

For Earth orbits:
| Altitude (km) | Radius (km) | Velocity (m/s) | Period (min) |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|--------------|
| 200 | 6,578 | 7,784 | 88.4 |
| 400 | 6,778 | 7,669 | 92.6 |
| 1,000 | 7,378 | 7,350 | 105.1 |
| 35,786 | 42,164 | 3,075 | 1,436 (24 hr) |

#### 1.3 Orbital Period

Orbital period:
T = 2π * √(a³/μ)

For circular orbits:
T = 2π * √(r³/μ)

Period at 400 km altitude:
T = 2π * √((6.778×10⁶)³ / (3.986×10¹⁴)) = 5,553 s = 92.6 min

#### 1.4 Orbital Energy

Specific orbital energy:
ε = v²/2 - μ/r = -μ/(2a)

For circular orbits:
ε = -μ/(2r)

Vis-viva equation:
v² = μ * (2/r - 1/a)

### 2. Orbit Types and Parameters

#### 2.1 Low Earth Orbit (LEO)
- Altitude: 200-2,000 km
- Period: 88-127 minutes
- Applications: ISS, Earth observation, communications constellations

Orbital decay from atmospheric drag:
da/dt = -ρ * v * (C_D * A/m) * a

Lifetime depends on altitude, area-to-mass ratio, and solar activity.

#### 2.2 Medium Earth Orbit (MEO)
- Altitude: 2,000-35,786 km
- Period: 2-24 hours
- Applications: GPS (20,200 km), navigation

#### 2.3 Geosynchronous Orbit (GEO)
- Altitude: 35,786 km
- Period: 23 hours 56 minutes (sidereal day)
- Orbital radius: 42,164 km
- Velocity: 3,075 m/s
- Applications: Communications, weather satellites

Geostationary requirements:
- Circular orbit (e = 0)
- Equatorial (i = 0°)
- Prograde (same direction as Earth rotation)

#### 2.4 Sun-Synchronous Orbit (SSO)

SSO precession rate = Earth's rate around Sun = 0.9856°/day

Required inclination (approximate):
cos(i) = -a^(7/2) * ω_ss / (1.5 * R_E² * J_2 * √μ)

Where:
- J_2 = 1.08263 × 10⁻³ (Earth oblateness)
- ω_ss = 1.991×10⁻⁷ rad/s

Typical SSO: 600-800 km altitude, 97-99° inclination

### 3. Orbital Maneuvers

#### 3.1 Hohmann Transfer

Minimum energy transfer between circular orbits:
Δv₁ = √(μ/r₁) * (√(2r₂/(r₁+r₂)) - 1)
Δv₂ = √(μ/r₂) * (1 - √(2r₁/(r₁+r₂)))

Total Δv:
Δv_total = Δv₁ + Δv₂

Transfer time:
t_transfer = π * √((r₁+r₂)³/(8μ))

LEO to GEO Hohmann transfer:
- Δv₁ (LEO departure) ≈ 2,440 m/s
- Δv₂ (GEO insertion) ≈ 1,470 m/s
- Total ≈ 3,910 m/s
- Transfer time ≈ 5 hours

#### 3.2 Plane Change

Inclination change Δv:
Δv = 2 * v * sin(Δi/2)

For small angle changes:
Δv ≈ v * Δi (radians)

Plane changes are expensive - minimize by launching at proper inclination.

#### 3.3 Station Keeping

GEO station keeping budget (annual):
- North-South (inclination): ~50 m/s/year
- East-West (longitude): ~2 m/s/year

LEO station keeping:
- Drag makeup: varies with altitude and solar activity
- 400 km ISS: ~2-4 m/s/year average

### 4. Spacecraft Subsystems

#### 4.1 Power System

Solar array power:
P_solar = η * S * A * cos(θ)

Where:
- η = Solar cell efficiency (20-30% for modern cells)
- S = Solar constant (1,361 W/m² at 1 AU)
- A = Array area (m²)
- θ = Sun angle

End-of-life degradation:
P_EOL = P_BOL * (1 - d)^(years)

Degradation rate d ≈ 2-3% per year

Battery sizing for eclipse:
C_battery = P_eclipse * t_eclipse / (η_battery * DOD)

Where DOD is depth of discharge (typically 30-40% for Li-ion).

#### 4.2 Thermal Control

Steady-state thermal balance:
Q_in = Q_out
α * S * A_sun + Q_internal = ε * σ * A_rad * T⁴

Where:
- α = Solar absorptivity
- ε = Infrared emissivity
- σ = Stefan-Boltzmann constant (5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²/K⁴)

Equilibrium temperature:
T = ((α*S*A_sun + Q_int) / (ε*σ*A_rad))^(1/4)

#### 4.3 Attitude Control

Gravity gradient torque:
T_gg = (3μ/r³) * (I_z - I_y) * sin(2θ)

Where θ is the angle from local vertical.

Reaction wheel sizing:
H_wheel > T_max * t_slew / 2

Momentum storage:
H_total = T_disturbance * t_orbit / 4

#### 4.4 Propulsion

Hydrazine thrusters:
- I_sp ≈ 220-230 s
- Thrust: 0.5-22 N typical
- Common for stationkeeping

Electric propulsion:
- I_sp: 1,500-3,000 s (Hall thrusters)
- I_sp: 3,000-10,000 s (ion engines)
- Thrust: mN range
- Power: 0.5-5 kW typical

Propellant mass:
m_p = m_dry * (exp(Δv/(g*I_sp)) - 1)

### 5. Communications

#### 5.1 Link Budget

Received power:
P_rx = P_tx + G_tx + G_rx - L_path - L_other

Free space path loss:
L_path = 20*log₁₀(4πd/λ) dB

For LEO at 400 km, 2 GHz:
L_path = 20*log₁₀(4π*400e3/0.15) = 162 dB

#### 5.2 Data Rate

Shannon capacity:
C = B * log₂(1 + SNR)

Required Eb/N0 depends on modulation and coding.
Typical: 3-10 dB for reliable communications.

#### 5.3 Antenna Sizing

Antenna gain (parabolic):
G = η * (π*D/λ)²

Beamwidth (3 dB):
θ ≈ 70λ/D (degrees)

### 6. Mission Design Considerations

#### 6.1 Coverage Analysis

Single satellite revisit time:
T_revisit ≈ T_orbit / cos(latitude)

Constellation sizing for continuous coverage:
N_planes * N_sats ≈ (2π * R_E) / (2 * swath_width)

#### 6.2 Lifetime Requirements

LEO lifetime factors:
- Orbital decay (altitude)
- Solar array degradation
- Battery cycle life
- Component reliability

Typical design lifetimes:
- CubeSats: 1-3 years
- LEO satellites: 5-7 years
- GEO satellites: 15+ years

#### 6.3 Launch and Deployment

Delta-V budget (example LEO mission):
| Maneuver | Δv (m/s) |
|----------|----------|
| Orbit insertion trim | 20 |
| Orbit raising | 50 |
| Inclination adjustment | 30 |
| Station keeping (5 yr) | 50 |
| Deorbit | 100 |
| **Total** | **250** |

### 7. CubeSat Standards

#### 7.1 CubeSat Sizes
- 1U: 10×10×10 cm, 1.33 kg max
- 2U: 10×10×20 cm, 2.66 kg max
- 3U: 10×10×30 cm, 4.0 kg max
- 6U: 10×20×30 cm, 12 kg max
- 12U: 20×20×30 cm, 24 kg max

#### 7.2 Typical CubeSat Power Budget

3U CubeSat power budget:
- Solar panels: 7-10 W average
- Battery: 20-40 Wh
- Payload power: 2-5 W
- Avionics: 1-2 W
- Communications: 1-5 W
- Attitude control: 0.5-2 W
