# Helicopter Powerplant and Transmission Systems
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Turboshaft Engine Fundamentals

#### 1.1 Operating Principles

Turboshaft cycle:
1. Air compression (compressor)
2. Fuel addition and combustion
3. Expansion through turbine
4. Power extraction via free turbine

Power output:
SHP = ṁ_air × c_p × (T_3 - T_4) × η_mech

Specific fuel consumption:
SFC = ṁ_fuel / SHP (lb/shp-hr or kg/kW-hr)

Typical SFC: 0.4-0.6 lb/shp-hr

#### 1.2 Engine Components

Compressor:
- Centrifugal: Simple, rugged, lower efficiency
- Axial: Higher efficiency, more stages
- Combined: Axial + centrifugal

Combustor:
- Can: Individual chambers
- Annular: Single chamber around shaft
- Can-annular: Combination

Gas generator turbine:
- Drives compressor
- Variable inlet guide vanes for control

Power (free) turbine:
- Mechanically independent
- Drives output shaft
- Allows rotor speed to vary

#### 1.3 Performance Characteristics

Power lapse with altitude:
SHP_alt/SHP_SL ≈ σ^0.7 to σ^0.9

Power lapse with temperature:
SHP_hot/SHP_std ≈ T_std/T_actual

Maximum continuous power (MCP): 100% rated
Takeoff power (TOP): 110-120% for 5-10 min
Emergency power: 130-150% for 30 sec - 2 min

### 2. Piston Engine Alternative

#### 2.1 Applications

Light helicopters:
- Robinson R22, R44
- Schweizer 300
- Lower cost, simpler maintenance

Typical engines:
- Lycoming O-360: 180 hp
- Lycoming O-540: 235-310 hp

#### 2.2 Power Characteristics

Power at altitude:
SHP_alt = SHP_SL × (ρ_alt/ρ_SL)^n

n ≈ 0.7 for naturally aspirated
n ≈ 0 up to critical altitude for turbocharged

SFC: 0.4-0.5 lb/hp-hr
Lower than turboshaft at low power settings

### 3. Transmission System

#### 3.1 Main Gearbox Functions

Speed reduction:
- Engine: 20,000-50,000 RPM
- Main rotor: 200-400 RPM
- Reduction ratio: 50:1 to 200:1

Power distribution:
- Main rotor
- Tail rotor
- Accessories (hydraulics, generators)

#### 3.2 Gear Types

Planetary gears:
- High reduction ratios
- Compact
- Power splitting
- Most common for final stage

Spiral bevel gears:
- Right angle drive
- Tail rotor takeoff
- Accessory drives

Spur gears:
- Simple, efficient
- Intermediate stages

Helical gears:
- Quieter than spur
- Axial thrust produced

#### 3.3 Lubrication

Oil system:
- Pressure: 40-80 psi
- Temperature: 60-120°C operating
- MIL-PRF-23699 (synthetic)
- MIL-PRF-7808 (legacy)

Oil consumption:
- Typical: 0.1-0.5 qt/hr
- Monitor for trends

Chip detectors:
- Magnetic plugs
- Warning of gear wear

#### 3.4 Transmission Rating

Continuous rating:
- Normal operation limit
- All-day capability

Takeoff rating:
- 5-10 minute limit
- 110-120% of continuous

Emergency rating:
- One engine inoperative (OEI)
- 2-30 minute limit depending on category

### 4. Tail Rotor Drive

#### 4.1 Drive Shaft

Long shaft from main gearbox to tail:
- Multiple sections with flex couplings
- Hanger bearings at supports
- Critical speed considerations

Critical speed:
N_crit = (π/L²) × √(E×I/(ρ×A))

Design above or below operating speed

#### 4.2 Tail Gearbox

90° bevel gear set:
- Changes rotation axis
- Some speed change

Typical tail rotor speed:
- 2-3× main rotor RPM
- Higher RPM, smaller diameter

Intermediate gearbox:
- Some designs have additional gearbox
- Allows drive shaft angle changes

### 5. Freewheeling Unit

#### 5.1 Purpose

Allows rotor to continue spinning when:
- Engine fails (autorotation)
- Engine slows during shutdown

One-way clutch:
- Sprag type most common
- Roller type alternative

#### 5.2 Sprag Clutch Operation

Engagement:
- Sprags tilt and lock
- Transmits torque engine → rotor

Overrun:
- Sprags straighten
- Rotor drives faster than engine
- No torque transmitted

### 6. Rotor Brake

#### 6.1 Function

Stops rotor after shutdown:
- Prevents wind-milling
- Safety during ground operations

Types:
- Disc brake on transmission
- Drum brake
- Multiple disc (wet)

Application:
- Only when rotor below safe speed
- Typically < 50% NR

### 7. Engine-Transmission Matching

#### 7.1 Power Requirements

Installed power:
P_installed ≥ P_hover_OGE × margin

Typical margins:
- Single engine: 20-30%
- Twin engine: One engine inoperative capability

#### 7.2 Twin Engine Considerations

Power sharing:
- Equal torque matching
- Load sharing controls

OEI ratings:
- 2.5 minute: 135-150% normal
- 30 minute: 120-130% normal
- Continuous: 100-110% normal

Category A operations:
- Continued flight capability
- After critical engine failure
- At critical decision point

#### 7.3 Altitude Effects

Hot and high conditions:
- Reduced air density
- Reduced engine power
- Increased power required

Design altitude:
- Specify hover capability
- Example: 6000 ft, 35°C, OGE

### 8. Auxiliary Systems

#### 8.1 Engine Starting

Starter-generator:
- DC motor for starting
- Generator after start

APU (large helicopters):
- Independent power source
- Ground operations
- Emergency power

Start sequence:
1. Engage starter
2. Fuel introduction at 15-20% N1
3. Light-off and acceleration
4. Starter cutout at 50-60% N1
5. Idle at 60-70% N1

#### 8.2 Fuel System

Fuel types:
- Jet A, Jet A-1 (turboshaft)
- 100LL Avgas (piston)

Fuel consumption:
- Turboshaft: 0.5-1.5 lb/shp-hr at cruise
- Piston: 0.4-0.5 lb/hp-hr

Fuel flow at hover (example):
- 2000 shp engine at 70% power
- SFC = 0.55 lb/shp-hr
- Flow = 0.55 × 1400 = 770 lb/hr = 115 gal/hr
