# Helicopter Rotor System Design
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Rotor Configuration Types

#### 1.1 Single Main Rotor with Tail Rotor

Most common configuration:
- Main rotor provides lift and propulsion
- Tail rotor provides anti-torque and yaw control
- Tail rotor power: 8-15% of main rotor power

Advantages:
- Simple, well-understood
- Efficient hover
- Good autorotation characteristics

Disadvantages:
- Tail rotor vulnerable
- Asymmetric in sideward flight
- Tail boom length and weight

#### 1.2 Tandem Rotors

Two main rotors in line (CH-47 Chinook):
- Counter-rotating to cancel torque
- No tail rotor needed
- Good for heavy lift

Rotor overlap typically 30-50%
Interference effects require careful design

#### 1.3 Coaxial Rotors

Two rotors on same axis (Ka-52):
- Counter-rotating
- Compact footprint
- Higher hover efficiency

Swashplate complexity doubled
Mechanical complexity high

#### 1.4 Intermeshing (Synchropter)

Two rotors that mesh (K-MAX):
- Tilted rotor shafts
- No tail rotor
- Good for external loads

Limited forward speed
Complex transmission

### 2. Blade Design

#### 2.1 Blade Planform

Rectangular:
- Simple construction
- Higher induced losses
- Common for smaller helicopters

Tapered:
- Root to tip taper ratio: 2:1 to 3:1
- Better hover efficiency
- More complex construction

Swept tips:
- Delay compressibility effects
- Reduce noise
- Parabolic or BERP-type tips

#### 2.2 Blade Twist

Linear twist:
θ(r) = θ_root + θ_tw × (r - r_root)/(R - r_root)

Optimal twist for hover: θ_tw = -8° to -15°
Compromise for forward flight: θ_tw = -8° to -10°

Non-linear twist:
- Hyperbolic distribution
- Better hover efficiency
- Manufacturing complexity

#### 2.3 Blade Airfoils

Root section:
- Thick (12-15% t/c)
- High C_L_max
- Structural depth

Mid-span:
- Moderate thickness (9-12% t/c)
- Low drag
- Good L/D

Tip section:
- Thin (6-9% t/c)
- High drag divergence Mach
- Supercritical profiles

Common airfoils:
- NACA 0012: Traditional, symmetric
- SC1095: Boeing advanced
- VR-series: Sikorsky designs
- OA-series: ONERA French designs

#### 2.4 Blade Construction

Spar types:
- D-spar (leading edge)
- Box spar
- C-spar

Materials:
- Aluminum: Traditional, fatigue limited
- Fiberglass: Damage tolerant, heavier
- Carbon fiber: Highest performance

Typical construction:
- Carbon/epoxy spar
- Nomex honeycomb core
- Fiberglass skin
- Erosion protection at leading edge

### 3. Hub Design

#### 3.1 Articulated Hub

Features:
- Flap hinge (out-of-plane motion)
- Lag hinge (in-plane motion)
- Pitch bearing (collective/cyclic)

Lead-lag dampers required
Complex but well-understood
Example: UH-60 Black Hawk

#### 3.2 Teetering (Semi-Rigid) Hub

Features:
- Teetering hinge for flapping
- No lag hinges
- Underslung for stability

Simple, lightweight
Two blades only
Example: Bell 206, Robinson R22

Coning angle:
β_0 = (γ/8) × (C_T/σ) × (1 + 8/3 × μ²)

#### 3.3 Hingeless (Rigid) Hub

Features:
- Blades attached rigidly to hub
- Flapping by blade bending
- High control power

Blade root highly stressed
Good handling qualities
Example: Bo-105, AH-64 Apache

Virtual hinge offset:
e = 0.1 to 0.15 R equivalent

#### 3.4 Bearingless Hub

Features:
- Flexbeam replaces hinges and bearings
- Composite construction
- Minimum parts count

Low maintenance
Complex analysis
Example: MD Explorer, Eurocopter EC135

### 4. Rotor Dynamics

#### 4.1 Flapping Equation

Equation of motion for flapping:
I_β × β̈ + M_β × β = M_aero + M_inertia

Natural frequency:
ω_β = √(1 + M_β_spring/(I_β × Ω²)) × Ω

For articulated rotor (no spring):
ω_β = Ω (1/rev)

For hingeless rotor:
ω_β = 1.05-1.15 × Ω

#### 4.2 Lead-Lag Motion

Lead-lag natural frequency:
ω_ζ = √(e × Ω² + k_ζ/I_ζ)

For articulated with hinge offset e:
ω_ζ ≈ √(3e/2) × Ω

Ground resonance:
- Coupling between lead-lag and fuselage
- Can be destructive
- Requires damping

#### 4.3 Blade Frequencies

Avoid resonance with:
- 1/rev (once per revolution)
- N/rev (N = number of blades)
- Higher harmonics

Fan plot (Campbell diagram):
- Frequency vs rotor speed
- Identify resonance crossings
- Place crossings outside operating range

### 5. Control System

#### 5.1 Swashplate Mechanics

Non-rotating portion:
- Connected to pilot controls
- Collective (up/down)
- Cyclic (tilt)

Rotating portion:
- Pitch links to blades
- Follows swashplate motion

Phase angle:
- Typically 90° lead
- Input at azimuth ψ produces response at ψ + 90°

#### 5.2 Control Mixing

Collective input θ_0:
- All blades change pitch equally
- Controls thrust magnitude

Longitudinal cyclic A_1s:
- Pitch varies sinusoidally
- Controls pitch attitude (after phase lag)

Lateral cyclic B_1s:
- Pitch varies sinusoidally (90° phase)
- Controls roll attitude

#### 5.3 Tail Rotor Control

Anti-torque:
T_TR × l_TR = Q_MR

Pedal input changes tail rotor collective:
- Right pedal: increase pitch, more thrust
- Left pedal: decrease pitch, less thrust

Some designs use:
- Fenestron (ducted fan)
- NOTAR (no tail rotor)

### 6. Vibration

#### 6.1 Sources

Blade passage frequency: N × Ω
- N-bladed rotor: N/rev vibration dominant

Rotor harmonics:
- 1/rev from imbalance
- N/rev from blade passage
- 2N/rev higher harmonics

#### 6.2 Reduction Methods

Blade tracking:
- Adjust blade pitch at rest
- Minimize track split

Dynamic balance:
- Add weights to rotor
- Minimize 1/rev

Vibration absorbers:
- Tuned mass dampers
- Active vibration control
- Isolation mounts

Higher harmonic control:
- Active blade pitch
- Reduces transmitted vibration

### 7. Performance Parameters

#### 7.1 Design Point Selection

Typical ranges:
| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| Disk loading | 150-600 N/m² |
| Blade loading C_T/σ | 0.08-0.12 |
| Tip speed | 180-220 m/s |
| Solidity σ | 0.05-0.12 |

#### 7.2 Blade Number Selection

2 blades: Simple, teetering, light helicopters
3 blades: Good compromise, less vibration
4 blades: Lower vibration, medium helicopters
5+ blades: Large helicopters, heavy lift

More blades:
- Lower vibration
- Higher solidity possible
- More complex hub
- Higher weight

### 8. Example Rotor Systems

#### 8.1 Robinson R22
- 2 blades, teetering
- Diameter: 7.67 m
- Solidity: 0.0347
- Tip speed: 205 m/s
- Disk loading: 112 N/m²

#### 8.2 Bell 206
- 2 blades, teetering
- Diameter: 10.16 m
- Solidity: 0.0463
- Tip speed: 214 m/s
- Disk loading: 186 N/m²

#### 8.3 UH-60 Black Hawk
- 4 blades, articulated
- Diameter: 16.36 m
- Solidity: 0.0826
- Tip speed: 221 m/s
- Disk loading: 431 N/m²
