# Helicopter Rotor System Design
## Reference Document for Aerospace Design Assistant

### 1. Rotor Configuration Types

#### 1.1 Single Main Rotor with Tail Rotor
- Most common configuration
- Tail rotor provides anti-torque
- Simple, well-understood
- Examples: Bell 206, Robinson R22, UH-60

Anti-torque power:
P_tail = 0.08-0.15 × P_main

#### 1.2 Tandem Rotors
- Two main rotors fore and aft
- Counter-rotating, no tail rotor
- High payload capacity
- Examples: CH-47 Chinook, Boeing Vertol

#### 1.3 Coaxial Rotors
- Two rotors on same axis
- Counter-rotating
- Compact, no tail rotor
- Examples: Ka-50, Ka-52

Interference effects:
Upper rotor: Reduced efficiency by 5-10%
Lower rotor: Operates in contracted wake

#### 1.4 Intermeshing Rotors
- Two rotors at angle
- Overlapping but not colliding
- Examples: Kaman K-MAX

### 2. Blade Design

#### 2.1 Blade Geometry

Aspect ratio:
AR = R / c_avg

Typical values: AR = 15-20

Solidity:
σ = N_b × c / (π × R)

Typical values: σ = 0.05-0.12

Blade taper:
c(r) = c_root × (1 - (1-λ) × r/R)

Taper ratio λ = c_tip/c_root = 0.3-0.5 typical

#### 2.2 Blade Twist

Linear twist distribution:
θ(r) = θ_root + θ_tw × (r/R - 0.75)

Typical twist: θ_tw = -8° to -15° (root to tip)

Optimum hover twist (ideal):
θ_opt = θ_tip × (R/r)

Non-linear twist for better forward flight performance.

#### 2.3 Blade Airfoils

Root section:
- Thick (12-15%)
- High lift capability
- Structural requirements

Tip section:
- Thin (8-10%)
- Low drag at high Mach
- Transonic performance

Common airfoils:
- NACA 0012: Symmetric, simple
- NACA 23012: Cambered, more lift
- VR-7, VR-8: Modern helicopter airfoils
- SC1095: Advanced transonic

#### 2.4 Tip Shape

Swept tips:
- Delay compressibility drag
- Reduce noise
- 15-30° sweep typical

Anhedral/dihedral:
- Influence blade vortex interaction
- Reduce noise

BERP (British Experimental Rotor Programme):
- Paddle tip shape
- Improved high-speed performance

### 3. Rotor Hub Design

#### 3.1 Articulated Rotor
- Flap, lag, and pitch hinges
- Reduces blade root loads
- Complex, heavy
- Examples: UH-60, AH-64

Hinge sequence:
Typically: flap → lag → pitch (outboard to inboard)

#### 3.2 Semi-Rigid (Teetering)
- Two blades connected rigidly
- Teetering hinge for flapping
- Simple, light
- Examples: Bell 206, Robinson R22

Underslung rotor:
Reduces 2/rev vibration

#### 3.3 Hingeless Rotor
- Flexible blade root
- Virtual hinges through bending
- High control power
- Examples: Bo-105, EC135

Equivalent hinge offset:
e_eff = 0.10-0.15 typical

#### 3.4 Bearingless Rotor
- Flex beams replace bearings
- Reduced maintenance
- Modern designs
- Examples: EC145, MD Explorer

### 4. Blade Dynamics

#### 4.1 Flapping Motion

Flap equation:
β̈ + ν_β² × Ω² × β = γ/8 × Ω² × (θ - λ/r)

Flap frequency ratio:
ν_β = √(1 + e_hinge × 3/2)

For articulated: ν_β ≈ 1.0
For hingeless: ν_β ≈ 1.10-1.15

Coning angle:
β_0 = γ × C_T / (8 × σ × a)

#### 4.2 Lead-Lag Motion

Lag equation:
ζ̈ + ν_ζ² × Ω² × ζ = f(Coriolis, drag)

Lag frequency ratio:
ν_ζ = √(3 × e_hinge / 2)

For articulated: ν_ζ ≈ 0.2-0.3
Must avoid ground resonance!

#### 4.3 Blade Natural Frequencies

Flap modes:
- 1st flap: 1.0-1.1 × Ω (articulated), 1.1-1.15 × Ω (hingeless)
- 2nd flap: 2.5-3.5 × Ω

Lag modes:
- 1st lag: 0.2-0.7 × Ω
- Must be separated from Ω

Torsion modes:
- 1st torsion: 3-6 × Ω typically

#### 4.4 Resonance Avoidance

Fan plot:
- Plot natural frequencies vs rotor speed
- Identify crossings with n/rev lines
- Avoid resonance in operating range

Critical resonances:
- Ground resonance: lag mode + fuselage
- Air resonance: lag mode + body pitch/roll

### 5. Rotor Performance

#### 5.1 Thrust Coefficient

Thrust coefficient:
C_T = T / (ρ × A × V_tip²)

Typical values:
Hover: C_T/σ = 0.08-0.12
Max: C_T/σ = 0.14-0.16 (stall limited)

#### 5.2 Power Coefficient

Power coefficient:
C_P = P / (ρ × A × V_tip³)

Component breakdown:
C_P = C_Pi + C_P0 + C_Pp

Induced: C_Pi = κ × C_T^1.5 / √2
Profile: C_P0 = σ × C_d0 / 8 × (1 + 4.65μ²)

#### 5.3 Figure of Merit

FM = C_T^1.5 / (√2 × C_P)

Or equivalently:
FM = Ideal power / Actual power

Target FM > 0.75 for good design.

### 6. Rotor Loads

#### 6.1 Blade Root Loads

Centrifugal force:
F_CF = ∫ Ω² × r × dm ≈ m_blade × Ω² × R/2

Typical: 10-20 times blade weight

Flap bending moment:
M_flap = ∫ (L - m×Ω²×r×β) × r × dr

Lag bending moment:
M_lag = ∫ (D + Coriolis) × r × dr

#### 6.2 Hub Loads

Thrust: T = N_b × ∫ L × dr

Hub moment:
M_hub = N_b × ∫ L × r × dr (articulated: at hinge)

Vibratory loads:
Primarily N_b/rev frequency

#### 6.3 Control Loads

Pitch link load:
F_pitch = M_pitch / r_arm

Where M_pitch is blade pitching moment about feathering axis.

### 7. Rotor Materials

#### 7.1 Blade Construction

Traditional:
- Aluminum spar
- Honeycomb core
- Fiberglass skin
- Stainless steel leading edge

Modern composite:
- Carbon/epoxy spar
- Nomex honeycomb core
- Carbon/glass hybrid skin
- Titanium or nickel leading edge

#### 7.2 Hub Materials

Steel:
- 4340 steel heat treated
- High strength, reliable
- Heavy

Titanium:
- Ti-6Al-4V
- High strength-to-weight
- Fatigue resistant

Composites:
- Bearingless hub flex elements
- Carbon/epoxy laminates

### 8. Design Example: Light Helicopter

#### 8.1 Requirements
- Gross weight: 1,200 kg
- Useful load: 400 kg
- Hover ceiling (OGE): 3,000 m
- Cruise speed: 200 km/h

#### 8.2 Initial Rotor Sizing

Disk loading selection:
DL = 200 N/m² (low for good hover)

Rotor radius:
R = √(W/(π × DL)) = √(11,772/(π × 200)) = 4.33 m

Tip speed:
V_tip = 210 m/s (moderate)

Rotor speed:
Ω = V_tip/R = 48.5 rad/s = 463 RPM

Solidity:
σ = 0.06 (2 blades, c = 0.41 m)

#### 8.3 Power Estimate

Hover power (FM = 0.75):
P_hover = T^1.5 / (FM × √(2×ρ×A)) = 175 kW

With tail rotor (+10%) and transmission (+5%):
P_total = 200 kW

Engine selection: 250 kW (margin for hot/high)
